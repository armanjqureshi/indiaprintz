GSM in T-Shirt Fabric: A Friendly Guide to Choosing the Right Weight

Confused by GSM in t-shirt fabric? See how 160, 180 and 220 GSM feel, which weight suits your event or team, and how to check the GSM of a tee at home.

By indiaprints and stitches · Updated 20 October 2026 · 15 min read

Hold two plain white tees side by side and they can look like twins, yet one feels feather light in your hand while the other drapes with a soft, reassuring weight, and the reason is almost always the GSM in t-shirt fabric. Same colour, same collar, same size label. Put them on and the difference is even clearer. One breathes like a summer kurta, and the other sits on your shoulders like a tee from a good brand.

That small number on a fabric spec decides how your printed tees feel, how they hang, how the print looks and how comfortable your team or crowd will be on the day. Most people only discover it after an order arrives a little too thin or a little too heavy for the occasion. You get to learn it now, before a single tee is printed.

At indiaprints and stitches, we have spent more than 8 years cutting, stitching and printing custom garments in our own unit, and GSM comes up in nearly every WhatsApp chat we have with customers. Some want "the thickest one you have", others want something that will not stick to the skin at a sports day in May. We genuinely enjoy helping people get this right, because the right fabric is what turns a printed tee into one people keep wearing.

This guide walks you through what GSM means, the common weight ranges, which GSM suits which use, how weight changes printing and price, and how to check GSM yourself at home. By the end you will know exactly what to ask for.

What GSM in t-shirt fabric really means

GSM stands for grams per square metre. Cut out one square metre of fabric, put it on a scale, and the number in grams is its GSM. A 160 GSM fabric weighs 160 grams per square metre, and a 220 GSM fabric weighs 220 grams.

That weight comes from how much yarn goes into the knit. Thicker yarn, a tighter knit or a denser structure all push the number up. This is also why two tees with the same GSM can feel slightly different, since one might use fine yarn knitted closely and the other a coarser yarn knitted more loosely.

One detail surprises a lot of people. GSM is measured on the fabric, not on the finished tee. An XXL in 180 GSM fabric weighs more than an S in the same fabric simply because it uses more cloth, longer sleeves and more stitching.

What GSM does not tell you

GSM is a weight, not a quality score. A heavy tee can be made from rough, uneven yarn that pills after a few washes, while a light tee in smooth combed cotton can stay soft and neat for years. A higher number only means more fabric per square metre.

It also says nothing about softness, shrinkage, colour fastness or how neatly the seams are stitched. Those come from the fibre, the finishing and the care that goes into stitching. So when you compare two quotes, ask about the fabric type along with the GSM, otherwise you are only comparing one part of the story.

T-shirt GSM chart: the common weight ranges

Here is a simple t-shirt GSM chart covering the ranges most buyers in India work with. Treat the borders as soft, because fibre and finish change how a weight feels in the hand.

GSM range Weight class How it feels Typical use
140 to 160 Light Thin and airy, can be slightly see-through in white and pastel shades Outdoor events, giveaways, running and sports tees
170 to 190 Everyday Opaque in most colours, holds its shape, comfortable all day Team tees, corporate tees, college fests, most printed orders
200 to 240 Heavy, premium Dense and structured, drapes with a satisfying weight Premium merch, retail-style tees, oversized fits
280 and above Fleece Thick, usually brushed or looped on the inside Sweatshirts and hoodies

If you remember one thing from this chart, make it this. For most printed orders, 170 to 190 GSM is the happy middle. It takes screen printing and DTF beautifully, never feels flimsy, and stays comfortable through most of the Mumbai year.

160 GSM vs 180 GSM vs 220 GSM

These three weights cover the bulk of what people ask us for, so here is how they compare once they are actually on your back.

160 GSM, light and breezy

A 160 GSM tee is light and breathes easily. It is a sweet pick for one-day events, marathons, beach clean-ups at Juhu and large giveaways where comfort in the heat matters more than how the tee looks after fifty washes. In white or pastel shades you may see a vest or a dark print through it, and a very large print can feel a little stiff on fabric this thin.

180 GSM, the everyday all-rounder

A 180 GSM tee is the one we find ourselves recommending most often. It is opaque enough for white, has enough body for a full front print, and does not feel heavy in a crowded hall without AC. If you are planning custom t-shirt printing and still feel unsure, this is a safe and very comfortable place to start.

220 GSM, the premium feel

A 220 GSM tee feels noticeably thicker and holds a structured shape across the shoulders and chest. This is the weight people mean when they say they want it to "feel like a brand tee". It is warmer to wear outdoors, so it shines for merch, gifting and evening or indoor wear rather than a sunny afternoon on the ground.

How fabric type changes the feel of the same GSM

Here is something experienced buyers learn early. The same GSM behaves very differently depending on what the fabric is made of and how it is finished. Our round neck tees can be stitched in cotton, polyester, sinker, polycot and bio-wash, polo t-shirts add matty to that list, and NSS polos also come in dry-fit. This is what to expect from each.

  • Cotton. Soft, absorbs sweat and takes most print methods well. At the same GSM it feels a touch heavier than polyester because cotton holds moisture.
  • Bio-wash cotton. Cotton given an enzyme wash that removes loose surface fibres. It feels smoother, pills less and often feels a little richer than plain cotton at the same weight.
  • Sinker. A cotton knit loved for its smooth face and soft hand. A popular choice for everyday printed tees in the middle range.
  • Polycot. A blend of polyester and cotton. It keeps its shape, wrinkles less and dries faster than pure cotton, which is handy for uniforms and tees that get washed every week.
  • Polyester. Feels lighter than cotton at the same GSM, dries quickly and is the fabric sublimation printing needs.
  • Dry-fit. A polyester knit made to pull sweat to the surface so it dries off. It usually sits in the lighter range and suits sports and activity wear.

A good rule of thumb is to choose the fabric first, for comfort and the print method you want, and then choose the GSM within that fabric. A 180 GSM polyester tee and a 180 GSM bio-wash cotton tee are two very different garments. Choosing the best GSM in t-shirt orders by use

There is no single best GSM for every printed tee. The right weight depends on where it will be worn, for how long, and what you want people to feel the moment they pick it up. Picture the day your tees will actually be worn, and the answer usually becomes obvious.

Outdoor events and Mumbai humidity

For walkathons, college sports days, Ganpati mandal volunteers or a stall at an open ground, go light. A 140 to 160 GSM cotton or polyester tee keeps everyone far more comfortable in sticky heat. If volunteers will be on their feet and sweating through the day, polyester or dry-fit dries faster and feels fresher by evening.

Team and corporate tees

For corporate t-shirts worn at offsites, town halls, product launches or Friday dress-down days, 170 to 190 GSM in cotton, bio-wash or polycot strikes a comfortable balance. People often commute in these tees too, so a mid weight that does not cling is the kindest choice. Polycot is worth a thought if the tees will be washed often.

Premium merch and gifts

For tees you plan to sell or hand to clients, 200 to 240 GSM bio-wash cotton gives the weight and finish people associate with a really good tee. The fabric holds a big print without looking stretched, and the tee keeps its shape wash after wash. This is the batch tee or brand tee that ends up as someone's favourite.

Oversized streetwear fits

Oversized tees with dropped shoulders need weight to hang well. At 160 GSM an oversized cut can look limp and tired. Most streetwear-style orders sit at 220 to 240 GSM so the shoulders and hem keep their structure.

Sports teams

For cricket, football and running, comfort and drying speed matter more than thickness. Lighter polyester or dry-fit is the usual choice, and it opens up full all-over designs for custom sports jerseys in your team colours.

Hoodies and sweatshirts

Hoodies and sweatshirts live in a different weight class altogether, usually fleece at 280 GSM and above. The inside is either brushed for a soft, warm feel or left looped for a lighter one. In Mumbai, hoodies get worn in chilly air-conditioned rooms, on monsoon evenings and on trips to Lonavala or up north, so the lower end of the fleece range suits city life, while heavier fleece is better for winter travel. How GSM in t-shirt fabric affects printing

Fabric weight changes how a print sits on the tee and how well it lasts. This is the part people often miss when they simply pick the lightest or cheapest option, so it deserves a few minutes of your time.

Light fabric and heat

Thin fabric moves more under a heat press and can pucker or show a faint shiny mark around the design. On light colours, a back print can show through from the front. Large, heavy prints on a 140 GSM tee can also feel stiff compared with the soft fabric around them.

Screen printing and DTF

Screen printing lays ink on top of the fabric, and a tee of 170 GSM or more gives it a firm, even base. This matters most on runs of about 50 pieces and above, where screen printing is the most economical per piece for 1 to 3 flat colours. DTF printing bonds a printed film to the fabric with heat and holds well on mid and heavy weights, which makes it ideal for photos, gradients and individual names on smaller quantities.

Sublimation needs polyester

Sublimation printing dyes the design right into polyester fibres, so it will not work on cotton at any GSM. It also needs white or light fabric. Choose polyester first and then the weight, usually on the lighter side for jerseys and kits.

Embroidery

Embroidery adds stitched weight to one spot on the garment. On thin fabric it can pull and wrinkle around the logo, so it looks its best on heavier tees, polos, shirts and hoodies.

How to check GSM yourself

You can measure GSM at home with a couple of simple tools. This comes in handy when you want to match a tee you already love or check what a supplier has sent you.

  1. Cut a 10 cm by 10 cm square from a single layer of plain fabric. Use an old tee or an offcut, and stay away from seams, ribbing and printed areas.
  2. Weigh the square on a scale that reads to 0.01 g. A small jewellery scale or a kitchen scale with two decimal places works well.
  3. Multiply the weight in grams by 100. A square weighing 1.8 g means the fabric is about 180 GSM.

The maths works because a 10 cm by 10 cm square is exactly one hundredth of a square metre. Fabric traders and factories use a round GSM cutter that punches out a 100 square centimetre disc in one press, and the calculation is the same.

During the humid months fabric holds moisture, so let your sample sit in a dry room for a while before weighing. A small variation is normal for knitted fabric.

If cutting a sample is not an option, compare by hand instead. Hold the tee you know next to the new one, feel the thickness between your fingers and look at both against a window. It is less exact, but it quickly tells you which one is lighter.

How GSM affects price

Heavier fabric uses more yarn per square metre, so a higher GSM tee usually costs a little more per piece than a lighter one in the same fabric. Fibre and finish play a part too, since bio-wash adds a processing step that plain cotton does not have.

A quick GSM checklist before you order

  • Where will the tee be worn: in the sun, indoors in AC, or as merch.
  • How long will people wear it in one go: a few hours or the whole day.
  • Which print method suits your design: screen, DTF, sublimation or embroidery.
  • If you need sublimation, the fabric has to be polyester.
  • Which fabric feels right: cotton, bio-wash, sinker, polycot or polyester.
  • Is the fit regular or oversized, since oversized usually needs 220 GSM or more.
  • Is the colour white or light, in which case avoid very light GSM for front and back prints.
  • What budget per piece works for you, including print positions.

Frequently asked questions

What is GSM in t-shirt fabric?

GSM means grams per square metre, the weight of one square metre of the fabric. A higher number means more yarn and a thicker, denser fabric. On its own it does not tell you about softness, shrinkage or overall quality.

Which GSM is best for printed t-shirts?

For most printed orders, 170 to 190 GSM works best. It gives screen printing and DTF a firm base, stays opaque in white and is comfortable in Mumbai weather. Go lighter for outdoor events and heavier for premium merch.

Is 180 GSM good for a t-shirt?

Yes, 180 GSM is the most popular all-round weight for printed tees. It suits team tees, corporate tees and college events. If you want a premium or oversized feel, look at 220 GSM instead.

Is higher GSM always better quality?

No. A higher GSM only means heavier fabric, not better yarn or better stitching. A well made 160 GSM combed cotton tee can outlast a rough 220 GSM one, so always check the fabric type along with the weight.

What GSM should I choose for oversized t-shirts?

Most oversized tees are made in 220 to 240 GSM. The extra weight helps the dropped shoulders and longer body hang with structure instead of looking limp.

What GSM is used for hoodies?

Hoodies and sweatshirts are usually made in fleece of 280 GSM and above. The lower end of that range is easier to wear in Mumbai, while heavier fleece suits colder travel and premium merch. Tell us the GSM you want and we will confirm availability.

How can I check the GSM of a t-shirt at home?

Cut a 10 cm by 10 cm square from plain fabric, weigh it on a scale that reads to 0.01 g and multiply the grams by 100. A 2.2 g square is about 220 GSM. You can also compare by feel against a tee whose GSM you already know.

Can I do sublimation printing on a cotton t-shirt?

No. Sublimation only works on polyester, and it needs white or light fabric. For cotton tees at any GSM, choose screen printing, DTF or embroidery instead.
