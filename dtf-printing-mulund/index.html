How DTF printing works, step by step

Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, DTF prints your design onto a special film first and then transfers it to the garment with heat and pressure. It sounds technical, but the process is easy to picture.

  1. Your artwork is checked and sized. We set the print size for each position and place it on a mockup for you to approve.
  2. The design is printed onto film. The colours go down first, followed by a layer of white ink that keeps those colours bright on dark fabric.
  3. A fine adhesive powder is added. It clings to the wet ink and is then cured, so the print can bond with the garment later.
  4. The film is heat pressed onto the garment. Heat and pressure fuse the design to the fibres.
  5. The film is peeled away. What stays behind is a crisp, full colour print with clean edges.

Because there are no screens or stencils to prepare, each piece can carry a different design. That is why DTF is the method we reach for when a list of thirty names arrives with thirty different sizes beside it. When to choose DTF, and when another method wins

DTF is a wonderful all rounder, but it is not always the cheapest or the best fit. Here is a simple way to decide.

DTF is your best pick when:

  • Your design has photos, gradients, shadows or more than three colours
  • You need fewer than about 50 pieces
  • Every piece needs a different name, number or nickname
  • You are printing on dark fabric or a mix of fabrics in one order

Another method may suit you better when:

  • You are ordering 50 or more pieces of a simple logo in 1 to 3 flat colours. In that case, screen printing in Mulund brings the per piece cost down nicely.
  • You want a stitched, premium logo on a polo, cap or jacket for front desk staff. Take a look at logo embroidery in Mulund for that finish.
  • You want an all-over design on white or light polyester jerseys, where sublimation dyes the colour right into the fabric.

Artwork and file tips for DTF printing

DTF can print almost anything you can see on a screen, so the quality of your file really shows in the final print. A few habits make a big difference.

  • Send the original file, not a forwarded copy. Images that travel through several WhatsApp chats get compressed. Share your file as a document so it arrives at full quality.
  • Choose a transparent background. A PNG with no background prints only the design. A white box behind your logo will print as a white box on a coloured tee.
  • Go big on resolution. Aim for an image that looks sharp when zoomed in at the size it will be printed. Vector files such as PDF, AI or SVG are ideal for logos.
  • Pick photos with good light. A bright, clear photo prints far better than a dim one. Faces in shadow stay in shadow on fabric.
  • Keep fine lines readable. Hairline strokes and tiny text can look faint on a textured fabric, so thicken them slightly if you can.
  • Type out names carefully. Send names as a typed list with the size next to each, spelled exactly as you want them printed.

Colours on a phone screen glow brighter than ink on cotton, so expect a small shift. The mockup helps you check placement, size and colours before anything is printed. For personalised orders, our guide to name printed t-shirts has handy layout ideas for fonts and placement.

How long DTF prints last, and simple wash care

A good DTF print feels smooth and flexible, stretches with the fabric and keeps its colour through regular wear when treated kindly. Mulund's long monsoon makes drying a challenge, so a little care helps your tees stay fresh for longer.

  1. Turn the garment inside out before washing to protect the print surface.
  2. Use cold or lukewarm water with a mild detergent, and skip bleach completely.
  3. Avoid long soaking. A quick wash is kinder to the adhesive layer than an overnight bucket.
  4. Dry in the shade or indoors under a fan during the rains, rather than over a hot heater.
  5. Iron inside out or with a cloth between the iron and the print. Direct heat on the print can make it sticky.

Follow these and your birthday tee will still be a family favourite at the next reunion.
