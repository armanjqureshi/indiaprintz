How sublimation printing works

Sublimation is part chemistry, part craft. The name comes from the way the ink turns straight from a solid into a gas, skipping the liquid stage. Here is what happens to your design.

  1. The design is printed in reverse. Your artwork is printed as a mirror image onto special transfer paper using sublimation inks.
  2. Paper meets polyester. The paper is laid on the white or light polyester garment, or on the cut fabric panels for full kits.
  3. Heat and pressure. A heat press brings the two together at high temperature. The ink turns into a gas and the polyester fibres open up to receive it.
  4. The dye locks in. As the fabric cools, the fibres close around the dye, trapping the colour inside the fabric itself.
  5. The paper comes off. What remains is a vivid design that you can see but cannot feel.

Because the dye bonds with polyester, the method does not work on cotton. It also cannot print white, since there is no white ink, so it is used on white or light coloured fabric. For more on the process, see our guide to sublimation printing in Mumbai.

When sublimation wins, and when another method is better

Sublimation is brilliant within its limits, and it is important to know them before you design.

Sublimation is the best choice when

  • The garment is polyester in white or a light colour
  • You want all-over patterns, gradients or photos edge to edge
  • The kit is for sport, running or anything sweaty
  • You want a print with zero feel that never cracks or peels
  • Every player needs a different name and number

Choose a different method when

If you want cotton tees, or dark coloured garments, sublimation will not work. For a bold logo on 50 or more cotton tees, screen printing in Malad is the economical route. For full colour designs on dark tees or hoodies, DTF does the job on almost any fabric. For a stitched crest on a team polo or cap, embroidery gives a classic finish.

Artwork tips for sublimation

Sublimation can reproduce almost anything, which is exciting, but a few file habits keep the result sharp.

  • Design in full colour freely. Gradients, textures and photos all print well.
  • Avoid white in the design. Any white area will simply show the fabric colour. On a white garment that is fine, but plan for it.
  • Send high resolution files. A large PNG or PDF at good resolution gives crisp edges, especially for big patterns.
  • Keep names and numbers bold. Thick, simple fonts read best from the boundary or the stands.
  • Leave space near seams. Very important details, like a logo, should sit away from the side seams and underarms.
  • Share the full player list early. Names, numbers and sizes in one simple list prevent mix ups.

If you only have an idea and a logo, send them over. We will help shape the layout and show it on a mockup.

Fabric pairing: why sublimation needs polyester

This is the one rule you cannot bend. Sublimation dye bonds only with polyester fibres. On cotton, the colour has nothing to hold onto and washes out quickly. That is why every garment in the table above is made in polyester for this method.

Colour matters too. The dye is transparent, so it shows best on white and gets duller on darker fabrics. White and very light shades give the brightest results.

The good news is that polyester is exactly what sports and outdoor gear should be made of. It is light, dries quickly and holds its shape, which is a gift in humid Malad weather and on sweaty turfs. If you want a specific weight, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Sublimated prints are about as durable as it gets. Because the colour is inside the fibres, there is no layer to crack or peel, and the design moves and stretches with the fabric.

A few habits keep the colours vivid:

  • Rinse kits soon after a match so sweat does not sit in the fabric.
  • Wash in cold water with a mild detergent.
  • Skip bleach and fabric softener, which can affect polyester.
  • Dry in the shade, since strong sun fades colours over time.
  • Avoid ironing at high heat, and if needed, use a low setting on the reverse.

Treat them well and your jerseys will still look bright at next year's tournament.

Can sublimation be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation only works on polyester, because the dye bonds with polyester fibres. For cotton tees, we recommend DTF or screen printing.

Can I get sublimation on a black jersey?

Sublimation shows best on white or light polyester, since the dye is transparent. For dark garments, DTF is usually the better choice.
