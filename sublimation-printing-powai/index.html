How sublimation printing works

Sublimation is a heat process with a bit of science behind it. Special dyes are printed onto paper, and when heated, they skip the liquid stage and turn straight into a gas. That gas bonds with polyester fibres and locks in as the fabric cools.

The process, step by step

  1. Print the transfer. Your design is printed in mirror image onto sublimation paper using special dye inks.
  2. Place it on polyester. The paper is laid face down on a white or light polyester garment or panel.
  3. Apply heat and pressure. A heat press brings the dye to a high temperature, turning it into gas.
  4. Dye enters the fibres. The gas moves into the polyester fibres, which open up slightly with the heat.
  5. Cool and lock. As the fabric cools, the fibres close and trap the colour inside.

Because the colour lives inside the fibres, there is no layer to feel, crack or peel. The fabric stays as light and breathable as it was before printing, which is exactly what players and runners want.

When sublimation is the right call, and when it is not

Sublimation is the best method when:

  • the garment is polyester in white or a light colour
  • you want an all-over or edge to edge design
  • the kit is for sport, running or the gym, where breathability matters
  • you want bright colours with nothing to feel on the surface

It does not suit every order. Sublimation does not work on cotton, and it cannot print onto dark fabric, because the dye cannot show up over a dark base. For cotton tees or dark garments with a full colour design, DTF printing in Powai is the better choice. For a smart team polo with a raised logo for the post-match dinner, embroidery in Powai gives that premium touch.

Designing artwork for sublimation

Sublimation can reproduce rich colour, gradients and photos, but it has a few quirks worth knowing.

  • White is not printed. Any white area in your design simply shows the fabric. On a white jersey that is fine, but plan for it if you choose a light coloured base.
  • Think about seams. For all-over designs, keep names, numbers and logos away from side seams, underarms and collar joins, where the pattern can break.
  • Use high resolution files. Design at full print size, at 300 DPI, or send vector files for logos and lettering.
  • Go bold with colour. Sublimation loves bright, saturated colours. Very dark, heavy areas can look slightly softer than on screen, so the mockup is your guide.
  • Send a clean names and numbers list. Include each player's name, number and size in one list, so every jersey is right.
  • Check the front and back together. Make sure stripes and patterns line up as you expect where the front meets the back.

If you have a rough idea but no designer, send a reference and your colours. The mockup will show you the full kit before anything is printed.

Fabric pairing for sublimation

This is the one method where fabric choice is not flexible. Sublimation bonds only with polyester, and it needs a white or light base.

  • Polyester jerseys are the classic pairing, light and quick drying for cricket and football.
  • Matty polyester polos suit teams that want a collared look for umpires, coaches or officials.
  • Polyester round necks work for run clubs and gym tees.
  • Polyester track jackets, shorts and caps extend the same design across a full kit.
  • Cotton, sinker and bio-wash will not hold sublimation dye, so pick DTF or screen printing for those.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Since the dye sits inside the fibres, a sublimated design will not crack, peel or rub off. It is one of the most durable ways to decorate sportswear, which is why so many teams choose it for their kits.

To keep colours vibrant across the season:

  • Wash in cold water with similar colours.
  • Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach.
  • Turn kits inside out to reduce rubbing.
  • Dry in the shade, as harsh sun can dull colours over time.
  • Avoid very hot irons, since high heat can affect the dye. A cool iron on the reverse side is enough.

Wash muddy kits soon after a match rather than leaving them in a sports bag overnight. Your jerseys will thank you.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Powai?

Sublimation starts at ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. Sports jerseys start at ₹200, so 16 jerseys printed front and back start at ₹4,000. Your final price is confirmed on WhatsApp once we see your design.

Can sublimation printing be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation only works on polyester, because the dye bonds with polyester fibres. For cotton tees with full colour designs, DTF printing is the right method.

Can I get sublimated cricket jerseys with names and numbers in Powai?

Yes. Send us each player's name, number and size in one list, and they will be built into the jersey design. The mockup lets you check every detail before printing.

Why does sublimation need white or light fabric?

Sublimation dye is slightly transparent, so it shows its true colour only on a white or light base. On dark fabric, the design would barely be visible.

What is the minimum order for sublimated jerseys?

Sports jerseys start at 5 pieces, which suits small box cricket sides as well as full squads. Shorts, polos, track jackets and caps also start at 5 pieces.

Does a sublimation print fade or crack?

It does not crack or peel, because the colour is inside the fibres rather than on top. With cold washes, no bleach and drying in shade, the colours stay bright for a long time.

Is sublimation or DTF better for a gym team's tees?

If the tees are light polyester and you want an all-over or breathable design, sublimation is ideal. For dark tees or cotton, DTF is the better choice because sublimation needs a light polyester base.
