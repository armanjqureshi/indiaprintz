How embroidery works, from logo to stitched badge

Embroidery turns your logo into thread, stitch by stitch. The process takes a little preparation, and the care that goes into those early steps decides how good the final logo looks.

  1. Your logo is digitised. We convert the artwork into a stitch file that tells the machine where each stitch goes, in which direction and in which thread colour.
  2. Thread colours are matched. We pick threads that are as close as possible to your brand colours and show them in the mockup.
  3. The garment is hooped with a backing. The fabric is stretched tight in a frame with a stabilising backing behind it, so the stitches lie flat and do not pucker.
  4. The design is stitched. The machine works through each colour, building up fills, outlines and lettering.
  5. It is trimmed and finished. Loose threads and extra backing are removed, leaving a clean, raised logo.

Because every logo is digitised from scratch, simple and bold logos give the best results. We will always tell you if a part of your design needs a small change to stitch well.

When embroidery is the best choice, and when to print instead

Embroidery is beautiful, but some designs and some garments are better printed. Here is a straightforward way to decide.

Embroidery is the right pick when:

  • You want a premium, textured logo on polos, shirts, caps or jackets
  • The garment will be worn and washed often, like staff uniforms
  • Your logo is reasonably simple, with clear shapes and readable text
  • You want the brand to feel polished at a reception desk, a visit or an event

Printing is better when:

  • Your design is a photo, has gradients or contains very fine text. These cannot be stitched, and DTF printing in Dombivli reproduces them in full colour.
  • You need a large back print, like a mandal name across a whole tee. Big designs are better and cheaper printed.
  • You are ordering 50 or more simple tees for an event. Screen printing in Dombivli gives the lowest cost per piece on big runs.
  • The garment is a thin, lightweight tee where dense stitching could pull the fabric.

Many Dombivli orders combine both. A factory might embroider supervisors' polos and screen print the shop floor team's tees with the same logo, so everyone matches while each group gets the right finish.

Preparing your logo for clean embroidery

A logo that looks great on a screen does not always translate directly into thread. These tips help your logo embroidery in Dombivli come out sharp and true to your brand.

Start with the cleanest file you have

A vector file like AI, PDF, SVG or CDR is ideal, since it shows every shape clearly for digitising. A high resolution PNG also works. A photo of a printed visiting card is the hardest to work from, so dig out the original file if you can.

Simplify very fine details

Very thin lines, tiny taglines and small text under the main logo often turn into a blur in thread. We may thicken lines slightly or suggest dropping a long tagline from a chest logo. You will see any change clearly in the mockup before we stitch.

Avoid gradients and photographic effects

Thread comes in solid colours, so soft gradients, shadows and photo effects cannot be stitched. A flat version of your logo, the kind you might use on a rubber stamp, is usually the best starting point.

Tell us your brand colours

Share the colour codes from your brand guide or a sample of your logo in print. We choose the closest thread shades and show them on the mockup.

Fabric pairing for embroidery

Embroidery likes fabric with some structure. Good pairings make the logo sit flat and look crisp for years.

  • Matty and cotton polos have a firm knit that holds stitches neatly, which is why polos are the most popular embroidered garment.
  • Polycot and cotton shirts stay smart through daily office wear and give a clean base for a chest logo.
  • Caps in cotton, polyester or polycot give a firm front panel that shows off a raised emblem.
  • Varsity jackets, zipper hoodies and track jackets have thicker fabric that suits larger crests and stitched names.

Very thin tees are better suited to printing. If you want a particular fabric weight for your polos or shirts, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for embroidered garments

Embroidery does not crack, peel or fade the way some prints can, which makes it a lasting choice for uniforms. A few habits keep the stitches looking neat.

  • Wash inside out to protect the stitches from rubbing.
  • Use a gentle cycle and a mild detergent, without bleach.
  • Iron on the reverse side, or place a cloth over the logo.
  • Do not pull at loose threads. Trim them carefully if one appears.
  • Dry caps in the shade so they keep their shape.

Treated this way, an embroidered polo can look neat on its hundredth wash, which is exactly what a staff uniform needs.
