What DTF printing is and how it works

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, we print your design onto a thin transfer film first and then press that transfer onto the garment. It sounds technical, but the journey from your file to your t-shirt is quite simple.

  1. Your artwork is printed in full colour onto a special film, with a layer of white ink behind the colours so they stay bright on black, navy or maroon fabric.
  2. A fine adhesive powder is spread over the printed film and cured with heat, turning the design into a ready transfer.
  3. The transfer is placed on the garment in the exact spot shown in your approved mockup, then heat pressed.
  4. Once it cools, the film is peeled away and the design stays bonded to the fabric.

Because no screens are made, there is no setup charge. DTF starts from ₹35 per print position, and that price holds for a single design or for 20 pieces that each carry a different name. Photos, gradients, fine outlines and dozens of colours all go down in one pass. Our main DTF printing page covers the method in more depth, and everything on it applies to orders from Thane too.

When to choose DTF, and when another method fits better

DTF is the right pick when your design is colourful, detailed or personalised, or when your quantity is on the smaller side. It is also handy when one order mixes garments, such as tees for the students and hoodies for the coaches, because the same transfer works on both fabrics.

Choose DTF when:

  • your design has photos, gradients, shadows or more than 3 colours
  • each piece needs its own name or number
  • you are ordering fewer than about 50 pieces
  • your order mixes fabrics, for example cotton tees and polyester jackets

Another method may serve you better in a few situations. For 50 or more pieces with a simple logo in 1 to 3 flat colours, screen printing in Thane brings the price per piece down. For a raised, stitched logo on polos, shirts and caps, embroidery in Thane gives a more premium finish that suits office wear. For all-over designs on white or light polyester jerseys, sublimation is the natural match. If you are deciding between the first two, our DTF vs screen printing guide compares them side by side.

Artwork tips that make DTF prints look their best

DTF reproduces almost anything, which also means the quality of your file shows up clearly on the fabric. A few small habits make a visible difference.

  • Send the highest resolution file you have. A PNG with a transparent background or a PDF works well, and a vector file (AI, EPS or SVG) is even better for logos.
  • Avoid screenshots or images forwarded several times on WhatsApp, since they get compressed and can print slightly soft.
  • Keep hairline strokes a little thicker than you think, because they can look faint once a design is sized down to a chest logo.
  • Share names as a typed list matched to sizes, in one message or one sheet, so every spelling is checked before printing.
  • If you want the fabric colour to show through part of the design, tell us, and we leave those areas empty on the transfer.

If you are unsure about your file, send it anyway. We check every file before preparing the mockup and tell you honestly if a cleaner version would print better.

Fabric pairing for DTF

One of the nicest things about DTF is how relaxed it is about fabric. Cotton tees, polycot uniforms, polyester track jackets, sinker hoodies and bio-wash tees all take it well, and the white underlayer keeps colours vivid on dark garments too.

For everyday comfort, cotton and bio-wash feel soft and suit academy and family tees. Polycot holds its shape through frequent washing, which makes it a good choice for staff tees worn several days a week. Polyester is light and dries quickly, so it works well for monsoon walks around Upvan Lake. For a heavier or lighter feel, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

One honest note. A very large DTF print covering most of the front sits as a smooth layer on the fabric, so it breathes a little less than a small chest logo. For long, hot days outdoors, a medium sized design often feels more comfortable.

Durability and wash care for DTF prints

A DTF print is flexible, so it stretches with the fabric instead of cracking when you pull a tee over your head. With a little care, it keeps its colour through regular wearing and washing.

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Use cold or lukewarm water and a mild detergent.
  • Keep bleach and strong stain removers away from the printed area.
  • Dry in the shade rather than harsh direct sun.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron inside out, or place a cotton cloth over the design.

Yes, photos are one of the things DTF does best. It prints full colour images, gradients and fine detail in a single pass. Send the original photo rather than a screenshot so it prints as sharply as possible.
