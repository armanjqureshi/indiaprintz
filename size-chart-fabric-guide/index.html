T-Shirt Size Chart and Fabric Guide for Custom Orders

T-shirt size chart in inches for S to XXL, how to measure a tee, collecting group sizes, and a fabric guide to cotton, polycot, sinker and dry-fit.

By indiaprints and stitches · Updated 20 October 2026 · 11 min read

This t-shirt size chart is the one we use for every custom order at indiaprints and stitches, so the sizes you pick here are the sizes that get cut and stitched. We are a cutting, stitching and printing unit in Dahanu, Maharashtra, and we take orders on WhatsApp and deliver by courier.

Most problems with group orders come from two things: wrong sizes and the wrong fabric for the job. Both are easy to fix before production starts and very hard to fix after.

This guide covers our size chart in inches, how to measure a tee, how to collect sizes from a team, and what each fabric we use feels like and which print methods suit it. It is written from 8+ years of making corporate and personal orders.

Our t-shirt size chart in inches

All measurements below are garment measurements, taken with the t-shirt laid flat on a table. They are not body measurements. Chest is the full chest of the garment, which is the flat width from armpit to armpit, doubled.

Size Chest (inches) Length (inches) Sleeve (inches)
S 36 26 8
M 40 27 8.5
L 44 28 9
XL 48 29 9.5
XXL 52 30 10

Each size step adds 4 inches to the chest, 1 inch to the length and half an inch to the sleeve. So an M laid flat measures 20 inches across the chest, and an XL measures 24 inches across.

This is the chart we work from for round neck tees on our custom t-shirt printing orders. If you need a size outside S to XXL, mention it on WhatsApp with the quantity and we will tell you what is possible for that order.

How to measure t-shirt size

The easiest and most accurate way is to measure a t-shirt you already own and like the fit of. Lay it flat on a table or bed, smooth out the wrinkles, and use a cloth measuring tape.

  1. Chest: measure straight across from one armpit seam to the other, about 1 inch below the armpit. Double this number. A tee that measures 21 inches across has a 42 inch chest.
  2. Length: measure from the highest point of the shoulder, right next to the collar, straight down to the bottom hem.
  3. Sleeve: measure from the shoulder seam to the end of the sleeve hem, along the top of the sleeve.

Now compare your three numbers with the chart. Chest matters most for fit, so pick the size whose chest is closest to yours. Then check the length, because a tee that fits the chest but sits too short is the most common complaint.

Garment measurement vs body measurement

A body measurement is taken around your chest with a tape. A garment measurement is the size of the t-shirt itself. The two are different, because a t-shirt needs some extra room so you can move and breathe in it.

If you measure your body and get 38 inches, you should not order a garment with a 38 inch chest unless you want it very tight. That is why we always suggest measuring a tee you own instead. It already includes the amount of room you are comfortable with.

Using the t-shirt size chart for a group order

For a team, class or event, collecting sizes properly is the part that saves the most trouble. We pack and label orders by size, so a clean size list also makes distribution at your end much faster.

A simple size list or spreadsheet

Share the size chart with everyone first, then collect sizes in one place. A Google Sheet or a plain WhatsApp list both work. Keep these columns:

  • Name
  • Department, class or team (helps when distributing)
  • Size from the chart (S, M, L, XL or XXL)
  • Name or number to print, if the design has one
  • Any note, such as "wants it loose"

Once the list is closed, send us the total count per size. For example: S 6, M 14, L 18, XL 9, XXL 3. If names are being printed, send the full list so each piece is matched to its size.

Common mistakes with group sizes

  • Guessing a split. Ordering "mostly M and L" without asking people almost always leaves some short of their size and others with extras nobody can wear.
  • Asking without the chart. People answer with the size they wear in a shop brand, which may not match our chart. Always share the chart with the question.
  • Collecting too late. If sizes are still coming in after the quote, the dispatch date moves. Close the list before you confirm.
  • No spares. For events, a few extra pieces in M and L cover late joiners and wrong choices.
  • Changing sizes after the mockup. Size changes after approval can affect production. Lock the list before approving.

Fit tips for oversized tees and women's fits

We do not use a separate chart for oversized or women's fits. The chart above is the one we work from, and the way to use it for a different fit is to compare it with a garment you already own.

Oversized look

For an oversized look, most people pick one or two sizes up from their usual size on our chart. The better way is to take an oversized tee you like, measure the chest and length flat, and pick the size on our chart that comes closest. Keep in mind that sizing up also adds length and sleeve along with width.

Women's fits

For women, the same method works best. Take a tee that fits the way you like, measure it flat, and match the chest number to the chart. Some prefer a closer fit and pick the size whose chest is just above their own tee, while others go a size up for a relaxed look. Measuring is far more reliable than guessing from shop sizes.

T-shirt fabric types we use

The fabric decides how a garment feels, how it handles Mumbai's heat and humidity, and which print methods will work on it. Here is a plain comparison of the fabrics we work with. For more detail on thickness, see our t-shirt GSM guide. Tell us the GSM you want and we will confirm availability.

Fabric What it is How it feels Print methods that suit it
Cotton Natural cotton fibre knitted into t-shirt fabric Soft, breathable, absorbs sweat Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Sinker A smooth knitted cotton fabric, common in good round neck tees Smooth, soft, slight stretch Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Polycot A blend of polyester and cotton Soft like cotton but holds shape better, fewer wrinkles Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Bio-wash cotton Cotton given an enzyme wash that removes loose surface fibres Smoother and softer than plain cotton, less pilling Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Polyester Synthetic fibre fabric Light, quick drying, keeps colour well Sublimation (white or light colours), DTF
Dry-fit Polyester fabric made to move sweat away from the skin Very light, dries fast Sublimation, DTF
Matty A textured fabric with a matt finish, used for polos Firm, structured, does not crease easily Embroidery, DTF
Canvas Thick woven cotton, used for tote bags Stiff, strong, holds its shape Screen printing, DTF, embroidery

Sublimation only works on polyester, so it is not an option on cotton, sinker or bio-wash. Screen printing gives the best results on cotton and blends. DTF works on most fabrics, which is why it shows up in almost every row. Our print methods page explains each one in detail.

Cotton vs polyester t-shirt for Mumbai weather

Mumbai is humid for most of the year, so this choice matters more here than in a dry city. Neither fabric is better for every use. It depends on where the t-shirt will be worn and for how long.

Cotton breathes and feels comfortable against the skin, which makes it good for office days, AC rooms and casual wear. The downside is that cotton absorbs sweat and holds it, so on a long day outdoors it can feel damp and show sweat patches.

Polyester does the opposite. It does not absorb much moisture and dries quickly, so it is better for sport, outdoor events, marathons and long hours in the sun. Plain polyester can feel less natural on the skin than cotton for all-day office wear.

Polycot sits in between. It has enough cotton to feel comfortable and enough polyester to hold its shape and dry a bit faster. For most mixed-use orders in Mumbai, polycot is a safe choice.

Which fabric to choose for each use

Here is how we usually guide customers, based on how the garment will be used.

Office and team t-shirts

For tees worn in the office or at a team day, cotton or bio-wash cotton is the usual pick. Bio-wash feels a step more finished and looks good with a screen printed or DTF logo. Sinker is another good option for a smooth, neat round neck.

Uniforms washed every day

Uniform t-shirts and polos that are washed daily need to hold their shape and colour. Polycot handles frequent washing better than pure cotton and wrinkles less. It is also one of the two fabrics, along with cotton, used for our school uniform sets.

Sport and outdoor

For sports teams and running events, polyester is the right base. It dries quickly and takes sublimation, which allows full-colour designs, names and numbers across the whole garment. See our custom jersey printing page for team kits. Dry-fit is offered on our NSS t-shirts, where volunteers spend long hours outdoors.

Premium merch

For merch that people will keep and wear by choice, go with bio-wash cotton or sinker. Both feel softer than basic cotton and take DTF well for detailed, full-colour artwork. A good fabric makes a bigger difference to how often merch gets worn than the print does.

Polos

For polos, matty gives a structured, office-ready look and pairs well with an embroidered logo on the chest. Cotton and polycot polos are also popular for a softer feel. Our polo t-shirt printing page lists the fabric options. For bags, canvas is what we use for totes, which you can see on our custom tote bags page.

Shrinkage and wash care

Cotton can shrink a little over the first few washes, especially in hot water or a hot dryer. Polycot shrinks less, and polyester shrinks very little. If you are between two sizes on a cotton order, going up one size is the safer choice.

Simple care keeps both fabric and print looking good for longer:

  • Wash in cold or normal water, not hot.
  • Turn the t-shirt inside out before washing, to protect the print.
  • Avoid bleach on printed or embroidered areas.
  • Dry in shade rather than strong direct sun, to keep colours from fading.
  • Do not iron directly on the print. Iron inside out or with a cloth on top.
  • Avoid hot tumble drying, especially for cotton.

Checklist before you confirm sizes

Run through this list before you approve the mockup and the size breakup:

  1. Everyone has seen the size chart before giving a size.
  2. Sizes are in one list with names, not spread across chats.
  3. Total count per size adds up to the full quantity.
  4. Names and numbers, if printed, are spelt correctly and matched to the right size.
  5. Fabric is chosen for how the garment will be used, with price as one factor.
  6. Print method suits the fabric, for example no sublimation on cotton.
  7. A few spare pieces are added if the group may grow.
  8. You have checked the mockup for logo size, position and colours.
  9. The delivery address and contact person are confirmed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the chest size of an M t-shirt in inches?

On our size chart, an M has a 40 inch chest, measured on the garment. Laid flat, it measures 20 inches from armpit to armpit. The length is 27 inches and the sleeve is 8.5 inches.

How do I measure my t-shirt size at home?

Lay a t-shirt that fits you well flat on a table. Measure across the chest from armpit to armpit and double it, then measure the length from the highest shoulder point to the hem. Match these numbers to our size chart.

Is the size chart for body or garment measurements?

It is for garment measurements, taken with the t-shirt laid flat. Your body chest measurement will be smaller than the garment chest you need. That is why we suggest measuring a tee you own instead of your body.

Which is better for Mumbai, cotton or polyester t-shirts?

Cotton is more comfortable for office and indoor wear because it breathes and feels soft. Polyester is better for sport and outdoor events because it dries fast. Polycot is a good middle option for mixed use.

What is the difference between bio-wash and normal cotton?

Bio-wash cotton has been given an enzyme wash that removes loose fibres from the surface. This makes it smoother and softer than plain cotton, and it pills less over time. It is a good choice for premium tees and merch.

What is sinker fabric?

Sinker is a smooth knitted cotton fabric used for good quality round neck t-shirts. It feels soft, has a slight stretch and takes screen printing, DTF and embroidery well.

Can I print a photo on a cotton t-shirt?

Yes, with DTF printing, which handles full colour, photos and gradients on most fabrics. Sublimation does not work on cotton, as it only works on polyester.

Do cotton t-shirts shrink after washing?

Cotton can shrink a little in the first few washes, mostly with hot water or hot drying. Washing in cold water and drying in shade keeps shrinkage low. Polycot and polyester shrink much less.
