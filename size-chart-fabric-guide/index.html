T-Shirt Size Chart and Fabric Guide for Custom Group Orders

Our t-shirt size chart in inches for S to XXL, how to measure a tee at home, how to collect sizes for a group, and which fabric suits Mumbai weather best.

By indiaprints and stitches · Updated 20 October 2026 · 13 min read

The box of team tees finally arrives, everyone grabs theirs, and within ten minutes you realise half the group never looked at a t-shirt size chart before telling you their size. One colleague is tugging at a hem that stops above the belt. Another is quietly asking if anyone wants to swap an L for an XL. The design looks lovely, but the moment you had pictured, everyone in matching tees and grinning for the group photo, has turned into a size swap at the reception desk.

The good news is that this is the easiest problem in custom apparel to avoid. A few minutes with the right chart, a tape and a simple list will get every person into a tee that actually fits. Picking the right fabric does the rest, so the t-shirt still feels good at four in the afternoon on a sticky Mumbai day.

We are indiaprints and stitches, and we have spent 8+ years cutting, stitching and printing custom apparel in our own unit. Thousands of corporate and personal orders have taught us exactly where sizing goes wrong and how to get it right the first time.

This guide gives you our exact size chart in inches, a simple way to measure at home, a stress-free method for collecting sizes from a group, and a plain-language fabric guide. By the end you will know what to order, in which sizes, and in which fabric.

Our t-shirt size chart in inches

These are the measurements we cut and stitch to, so the size you choose here is the size that arrives at your door. All numbers are garment measurements, taken with the t-shirt laid flat on a table. They are not body measurements. Chest is the full chest of the garment, which means the flat width from armpit to armpit, doubled.

Size Chest (inches) Length (inches) Sleeve (inches)
S 36 26 8
M 40 27 8.5
L 44 28 9
XL 48 29 9.5
XXL 52 30 10

Each step up adds 4 inches to the chest, 1 inch to the length and half an inch to the sleeve. So an M laid flat measures 20 inches across the chest, and an XL measures 24 inches across. That simple pattern makes it easy to see how much roomier the next size really is.

This is the chart we work from for round neck tees on our custom t-shirt printing orders. If someone in your group needs a size outside S to XXL, just mention it on WhatsApp along with the quantity, and we will tell you what is possible for that order.

How to measure your t-shirt size at home

Here is our favourite trick, and it works every time. Find a t-shirt you already own and love the fit of, lay it flat on a bed or table, smooth out the wrinkles, and grab a cloth measuring tape.

  1. Chest: measure straight across from one armpit seam to the other, about 1 inch below the armpit. Double the number. A tee that measures 21 inches across has a 42 inch chest.
  2. Length: measure from the highest point of the shoulder, right next to the collar, straight down to the bottom hem.
  3. Sleeve: measure from the shoulder seam to the end of the sleeve hem, along the top of the sleeve.

Now hold your three numbers up against the chart. Chest matters most for fit, so pick the size whose chest is closest to yours. Then check the length, because a tee that fits the chest but sits too short is the complaint we hear most often.

Garment measurement vs body measurement

A body measurement is taken around your chest with a tape. A garment measurement is the size of the t-shirt itself. They are never the same number, because a t-shirt needs a little extra room so you can move, lift your arms and breathe comfortably.

If your body measures 38 inches, a garment with a 38 inch chest will feel very snug. That is exactly why we suggest measuring a tee you already own. It quietly includes the amount of ease you like, so the match is far more reliable.

Using the t-shirt size chart for a group order

This is where the magic happens for teams, classes, societies and event crews. A clean size list is the single biggest thing that separates a happy handout from a day of swaps. Since we pack and label every order by size, a good list also makes distribution at your end wonderfully quick.

Build a simple size list

Share the chart with everyone first, then collect sizes in one place. A Google Sheet works beautifully, and so does a plain WhatsApp list if your group is small. Keep these columns:

  • Name
  • Department, class or team, which helps when you hand things out
  • Size from our chart (S, M, L, XL or XXL)
  • Name or number to print, if your design has one
  • Any note, such as "prefers a loose fit"

Once the list is closed, send us the total count per size, for example S 6, M 14, L 18, XL 9 and XXL 3. If names or numbers are being printed, send the full list so every piece is matched to the right person and the right size. We are always happy to receive a size list exactly as you collected it.

Common mistakes with group sizes

  • Guessing the split. Ordering "mostly M and L" without asking people almost always leaves a few without their size and a few extras nobody can wear.
  • Asking without the chart. People reply with the size they wear in their usual brand, which may not match ours. Always send the chart along with the question.
  • Collecting too late. If sizes keep trickling in after the quote, the dispatch date moves. Close the list before you confirm.
  • Forgetting spares. For events, a few extra pieces in M and L cover late joiners and last-minute changes of mind.
  • Changing sizes after the mockup. Lock the list before you approve, so production can start smoothly.

Fit tips for oversized tees and women's fits

We work from the one chart above for every fit. The way to use it for a different look is to compare it with a garment you already own and love.

Oversized look

For that relaxed, oversized feel, most people go one or two sizes up from their usual size on our chart. The smarter way is to measure an oversized tee you like, chest and length flat, and pick the size on our chart that comes closest. Remember that sizing up adds length and sleeve along with width.

Women's fits

The same method works best here too. Take a tee that fits the way you like, measure it flat, and match the chest number to the chart. Some prefer a closer fit and pick the size whose chest sits just above their own tee, while others happily go a size up for an easy, casual look. A quick measurement beats guessing every time.

A friendly guide to t-shirt fabric types

Fabric decides how your tee feels, how it copes with Mumbai's heat and humidity, and which print methods will look their best on it. Here is a plain comparison of the fabrics we work with. For more on thickness and weight, our t-shirt GSM guide explains it all, and you can tell us the GSM you want so we can confirm availability.

Fabric What it is How it feels Print methods that suit it
Cotton Natural cotton fibre knitted into t-shirt fabric Soft, breathable, absorbs sweat Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Sinker A smooth knitted cotton fabric, common in good round neck tees Smooth, soft, slight stretch Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Polycot A blend of polyester and cotton Soft like cotton but holds shape better, fewer wrinkles Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Bio-wash cotton Cotton given an enzyme wash that removes loose surface fibres Smoother and softer than plain cotton, less pilling Screen printing, DTF, embroidery
Polyester Synthetic fibre fabric Light, quick drying, keeps colour well Sublimation (white or light colours), DTF
Dry-fit Polyester fabric made to move sweat away from the skin Very light, dries fast Sublimation, DTF
Matty A textured fabric with a matt finish, used for polos Firm, structured, does not crease easily Embroidery, DTF
Canvas Thick woven cotton, used for tote bags Stiff, strong, holds its shape Screen printing, DTF, embroidery

A quick rule of thumb: sublimation only works on polyester, so it is not an option on cotton, sinker or bio-wash. Screen printing shines on cotton and blends, and DTF works on most fabrics, which is why it appears in almost every row. Our print methods page walks you through each one.

Cotton vs polyester t-shirt for Mumbai weather

Mumbai is humid for most of the year, so this choice matters more here than in a dry city. Neither fabric wins every time. It all depends on where the tee will be worn and for how long.

Cotton breathes and feels lovely against the skin, which makes it perfect for workdays, AC rooms and casual wear. The catch is that cotton soaks up sweat and holds on to it, so after a long day outdoors it can feel damp and show patches.

Polyester does the opposite. It absorbs very little moisture and dries quickly, so it is the better pick for sport, outdoor events, marathons and long hours in the sun. Plain polyester can feel a little less natural than cotton for all-day wear indoors.

Polycot sits comfortably in between. It has enough cotton to feel soft and enough polyester to hold its shape and dry a bit faster. For most mixed-use orders in Mumbai, polycot is a safe and happy choice.

Which fabric to choose for each use

Here is how we usually guide people, based on how the garment will actually be worn.

Team and workplace tees

For tees worn at work or on a team day, cotton or bio-wash cotton is the usual favourite. Bio-wash feels a step more finished and looks sharp with a screen printed or DTF logo. Sinker is another lovely option for a smooth, neat round neck.

Uniforms washed every day

Uniform tees and polos that go through the wash daily need to hold their shape and colour. Polycot handles frequent washing better than pure cotton and wrinkles less. It is also one of the two fabrics, along with cotton, used for our school uniform sets.

Sport and outdoor

For sports teams and running events, polyester is the right base. It dries fast and takes sublimation, which means full-colour designs, names and numbers across the whole garment. Our custom jersey printing page covers team kits in detail. Dry-fit is offered on our NSS t-shirts, where volunteers spend long hours outdoors.

Premium merch

For merch that people will reach for by choice, go with bio-wash cotton or sinker. Both feel softer than basic cotton and take DTF beautifully for detailed, full-colour artwork. A good fabric is often the reason a batch tee becomes the one someone wears for years.

Polos

For polos, matty gives a structured, smart look and pairs wonderfully with an embroidered logo on the chest. Cotton and polycot polos are popular for a softer feel. You will find all the fabric options on our polo t-shirt printing page.

Shrinkage and wash care

Cotton can shrink a little over the first few washes, especially in hot water or a hot dryer. Polycot shrinks less, and polyester barely at all. If someone is between two sizes on a cotton order, going up one size is the safer choice.

A little care keeps both the fabric and the print looking fresh for longer:

  • Wash in cold or normal water, not hot.
  • Turn the t-shirt inside out before washing to protect the print.
  • Avoid bleach on printed or embroidered areas.
  • Dry in shade rather than strong direct sun to keep colours bright.
  • Do not iron directly on the print. Iron inside out or with a cloth on top.
  • Skip hot tumble drying, especially for cotton.

Checklist before you confirm sizes

Run through this quick list before you approve the mockup and the size breakup:

  1. Everyone has seen the size chart before giving a size.
  2. Sizes are in one list with names, not scattered across chats.
  3. The total count per size adds up to the full quantity.
  4. Names and numbers, if printed, are spelt correctly and matched to the right size.
  5. The fabric suits how the garment will be used.
  6. The print method suits the fabric, for example no sublimation on cotton.
  7. A few spare pieces are added if the group may grow.
  8. You have checked the mockup for logo size, position and colours.
  9. The delivery address and contact person are confirmed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the chest size of an M t-shirt in inches?

On our size chart, an M has a 40 inch chest, measured on the garment. Laid flat, it measures 20 inches from armpit to armpit. The length is 27 inches and the sleeve is 8.5 inches.

How do I measure my t-shirt size at home?

Lay a t-shirt that fits you well flat on a table. Measure across the chest from armpit to armpit and double it, then measure the length from the highest shoulder point to the hem. Match these numbers to our size chart.

Is the t-shirt size chart for body or garment measurements?

It is for garment measurements, taken with the t-shirt laid flat. The garment chest you need will be larger than your body chest measurement. That is why we suggest measuring a tee you own instead of your body.

Which is better for Mumbai, cotton or polyester t-shirts?

Cotton is more comfortable for indoor and everyday wear because it breathes and feels soft. Polyester is better for sport and outdoor events because it dries fast. Polycot is a good middle option for mixed use.

How do I order custom t-shirts in different sizes for a group?

Share our size chart with the group, collect everyone's size in one list, and send us the count per size on WhatsApp. If names are printed, send the full list so each piece matches its size. Your order arrives packed and labelled by size.

What is the difference between bio-wash and normal cotton?

Bio-wash cotton has been given an enzyme wash that removes loose fibres from the surface. This makes it smoother and softer than plain cotton, and it pills less over time. It is a great choice for premium tees and merch.

Can I print a photo on a cotton t-shirt?

Yes, with DTF printing, which handles full colour, photos and gradients on most fabrics. Sublimation does not work on cotton, as it only works on polyester.

Do cotton t-shirts shrink after washing?

Cotton can shrink a little in the first few washes, mostly with hot water or hot drying. Washing in cold water and drying in shade keeps shrinkage low. Polycot and polyester shrink much less.
