How sublimation printing works

Sublimation uses heat to turn special ink into a gas that bonds with polyester fibres. When it cools, the colour is locked inside the fabric itself.

  1. Your design is prepared. We lay out the front, back and sleeves with names and numbers in place, then send you a mockup.
  2. The design is printed onto transfer paper. Sublimation inks are used, and the image is printed in reverse.
  3. Paper and fabric go into a heat press. High heat and pressure turn the ink into a gas.
  4. The gas bonds with the polyester. The colour moves into the fibres rather than resting on the surface.
  5. The fabric cools and the colour is locked in. For all-over designs, the printed panels are then cut and stitched into finished garments at our unit.

Because the ink becomes part of the fibre, there is no layer on top to feel. Run your hand over a sublimated jersey and it feels exactly like plain fabric. Our main page on sublimation printing has more on how the method works across our range.

When sublimation is the right call, and when it is not

Sublimation is brilliant, but it has firm rules. Knowing them early avoids disappointment.

Pick sublimation when:

  • Your garment is polyester and your base fabric is white or light
  • You want an all-over design, stripes that run edge to edge, or patterns across sleeves
  • You need many colours and photographic detail on sports wear
  • Comfort and breathability matter, because there is no ink layer on top

Pick another method when:

  • You want cotton tees or hoodies. Sublimation does not work on cotton, so DTF printing in Mulund is the full colour alternative there.
  • You need a dark base fabric such as a black or navy polyester tee. Sublimation cannot print light colours onto dark fabric.
  • You want a stitched crest on a cap or a coach's jacket. For that premium touch, look at embroidery in Mulund.

One helpful tip is that a jersey can still look dark. Because the design covers the whole panel, we can print deep blues, blacks and maroons onto white polyester, so your kit looks rich even though the base fabric is white.

Design and file tips for sublimation

Sublimation can print almost unlimited colours, so it rewards bold, creative designs. These tips help your kit look its best.

  • Design for the whole garment. Think of the front, back and sleeves as one canvas so patterns flow across the seams naturally.
  • Keep key details away from the seams. Names, numbers and logos look best on the flat centre of each panel.
  • Use high resolution artwork. Large prints show every flaw, so send vector files or very large images.
  • Send a typed names and numbers list. Put each player's name, number and size on one line, spelled exactly as you want it.
  • Choose bright, saturated colours. Sublimation makes colours vivid, and strong contrast between the number and the background keeps it readable from the boundary.
  • Share your club colours. A colour code or a photo of last season's kit helps us match the shades closely.

If you only have a rough sketch or a reference photo, that is fine. Share it on WhatsApp and we will help turn it into a full mockup for you to approve.

Fabric pairing for sublimation

Sublimation has one golden rule: polyester only, in white or light shades.

  • Polyester jersey fabric is the classic choice for cricket and football kits. It is light, breathable and quick drying.
  • Dry-fit style polyester keeps runners and cyclists comfortable through long, humid mornings.
  • Polyester shorts match the jersey perfectly so the kit looks like one set.
  • Polyester track jackets in light shades can carry team colours and sponsor style panels.

Cotton, sinker and cotton blends will not hold sublimation ink, so we suggest DTF or screen printing for those. Tell us the GSM you want for your jerseys and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Since the colour lives inside the fibres, a sublimated jersey does not crack, peel or feel heavy. It stays soft and comfortable through a long season. A few habits keep the colours bright.

  1. Wash soon after a match so sweat and mud do not sit in the fabric for days.
  2. Use cold water and a mild detergent. Hot water is unnecessary for polyester.
  3. Keep bleach and harsh stain removers away, as they can dull the colours.
  4. Line dry in the shade. Strong sun over many months can fade any colour.
  5. Do not iron at high heat. Polyester barely creases, and a cool iron is plenty if needed.

Treated well, your team kit will look just as bright in the final as it did in the opening match.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Mulund?

Sublimation starts from ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. A sports jersey starts at ₹200, so a jersey with a sublimated front and back starts at ₹250. Your exact quote comes on WhatsApp once we see the design and squad size.

Can sublimation printing be done on cotton t-shirts?

No, sublimation only works on polyester. For full colour designs on cotton, we recommend DTF printing, which works on most fabrics.

Can I get a black sublimated jersey?

Sublimation needs a white or light base fabric. However, the design can cover the whole jersey in deep colours like black, navy or maroon, so the finished kit can still look dark.

What is the minimum order for sublimation jerseys in Mulund?

Sports jerseys, shorts and most polyester garments start from 5 pieces. That makes sublimation easy even for a small box cricket or turf football squad.

Can every jersey have a different name and number?

Yes. Send a typed list with each player's name, number and size, and every jersey is printed individually. You will see the layout on the mockup before production.

Will sublimated jerseys fade or crack after washing?

Sublimated prints do not crack or peel because the colour is inside the fabric. Washing in cold water, avoiding bleach and drying in the shade keeps the colours bright for a long time.

Is sublimation cheaper than DTF for polyester jerseys?

On polyester, sublimation starts at ₹25 per position compared with ₹35 for DTF, and it can cover the whole garment. For jerseys and team kits on light polyester, it is usually the better value.
