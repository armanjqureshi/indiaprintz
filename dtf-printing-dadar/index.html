What DTF printing is, in plain words

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, we first print your design onto a thin, clear film and then transfer it to the garment with heat. It sounds technical, but the steps are easy to follow.

  1. Your artwork is printed in full colour onto the film, exactly as it appears in your file.
  2. A layer of white ink goes down behind the colours, so they stay bright even on a black or navy tee.
  3. A fine adhesive powder is spread over the wet print and cured, which is what helps it grip the fabric.
  4. The film is placed on the garment in the right position and pressed with heat.
  5. Once it cools, the film is peeled away and the print stays bonded to the fabric.

Because there are no screens to prepare, a design with two colours costs the same to print as one with two hundred. That is why DTF is so friendly to small orders, photographs and tees where every piece carries a different name.

When to pick DTF, and when another method is better

We will always tell you honestly which method gives the best result for your money. DTF is our first suggestion when:

  • your design has photos, gradients, shading or many colours,
  • you need fewer than about 50 pieces,
  • each piece needs a different name or number,
  • you are mixing fabrics in one order, such as cotton tees and polyester hoodies.

Other methods win in other situations. For 50 or more pieces with a simple design in 1 to 3 flat colours, screen printing in Dadar usually works out cheaper per piece. For a premium stitched logo on polos, shirts, caps or jackets, embroidery in Dadar gives a more formal finish. And for polyester sports jerseys with all-over patterns, sublimation is the better fit because it dyes the design into the fabric.

Artwork tips that help DTF prints look their best

DTF prints what is in your file, very faithfully. So a little care with the artwork pays off.

  • Send the highest resolution you have. A PNG at 300 dpi at the size you want printed is ideal. A small image saved from a chat or social media may look fine on a phone but print soft on a tee.
  • Use a transparent background. If your design sits on a white or coloured box in the file, that box will print too. Ask your designer for a PNG with no background, or send us the original and we will check it.
  • Watch very thin lines and tiny text. Hairline strokes and very small lettering can lose detail. Keep fine text a touch bolder than you think it needs to be.
  • Choose sharp, well-lit photos. For family photos, the clearer the original, the better the print. An old print scanned flat at high resolution beats a phone photo taken at an angle.
  • Send names as a typed list. Give the name, size and any number for each person in a message or spreadsheet. Our guide to name printed t-shirts has more on layouts.

Screens and fabric show colour slightly differently, so the mockup is your moment to check that the colours feel right. If you have exact brand colours, share the codes and we will match them as closely as the method allows.

Fabric pairing for DTF

One of DTF's biggest strengths is that it works on most fabrics. We use it on cotton, polyester, sinker, polycot, bio-wash and matty polos, as well as canvas totes. Light and dark garments are both fine, thanks to the white layer behind the colours.

There is one thing to keep in mind about feel. A DTF print sits as a thin, smooth layer on top of the fabric. On a chest logo you barely notice it, but a very large solid block of colour across the back will feel a little heavier and less breathable. If your design allows it, breaking a big solid area into shapes, or letting the fabric show through, keeps the tee comfortable on a humid Mumbai afternoon.

If you have a fabric weight in mind, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability with the quote.

Durability and wash care for DTF prints

A well-pressed DTF print is stretchy and holds up nicely to regular wear and washing. Like any print, it lasts longest when you treat it kindly.

  • Wait a day before the first wash.
  • Turn the garment inside out.
  • Wash in cold or lukewarm water with a mild detergent, and skip the bleach.
  • Dry in the shade rather than harsh direct sun, and avoid high-heat tumble drying.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron inside out, or place a cloth over it.

Follow these and your fest tee will still look good long after the fest is over, which is exactly what you want from a souvenir.
