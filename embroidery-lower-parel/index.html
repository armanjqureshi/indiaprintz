How logo embroidery in Lower Parel works, step by step

Embroidery turns your flat logo into a stitched design. It needs a little more preparation than printing, which is a big part of why it looks so polished.

  1. Digitising the logo. Your artwork is converted into a stitch file. This file tells the embroidery machine where every stitch goes, in what direction, in which colour and in what order.
  2. Choosing thread colours. We match the threads to your brand colours as closely as possible and show you the result on the mockup.
  3. Hooping the garment. The polo, shirt or cap is clamped firmly in a frame with a stabilising backing behind the fabric, so it does not pucker while being stitched.
  4. Stitching. The machine sews the design colour by colour, building up fills, outlines and lettering.
  5. Finishing. Loose threads are trimmed, extra backing is removed from the inside, and each piece is checked before packing.

Because the stitch file is made once for your logo, adding more pieces later is straightforward. Many offices start with one team and reorder for new joiners with exactly the same look.

Embroidery or print: picking the right finish

Embroidery is a wonderful finish, but it is not the answer to every design. We would rather tell you honestly when a print will serve you better.

Embroidery is the right choice when:

  • You want a premium, textured logo for office wear or gifting
  • The design is a logo, a monogram or short lettering
  • The garment is a polo, shirt, cap, hoodie or jacket
  • You want a finish that lasts for years of washing

Embroidery is not suited to very fine text, tiny details or photographs. Those simply cannot be reproduced in thread. If your design has a photo, gradients or a large, colourful illustration, DTF printing in Lower Parel will capture it far better. For a big run of simple event tees, screen printing is the budget friendly option. Many teams combine methods, for example an embroidered logo on the chest with a printed design on the back.

Getting your logo ready for embroidery

The better your starting file, the cleaner your embroidered logo. These tips help.

  • Send a vector or high resolution file. AI, EPS, SVG, PDF or a large PNG lets us digitise accurately.
  • Keep lettering readable. Very small text turns into a blur in thread. As a rough guide, letters should be at least about 5 mm tall on a chest logo.
  • Simplify fine details. Hairline strokes, tiny dots and soft shadows do not translate into stitches. We may suggest a slightly simplified version of your logo for embroidery.
  • Limit colours where you can. Embroidery handles several thread colours, but a logo with 2 to 4 clear colours usually looks the sharpest.
  • No photos or gradients. These cannot be embroidered. A solid colour logo is the ideal starting point.
  • Share your brand colours. Pantone or hex codes help us pick the closest thread shades.

If your logo needs a small tweak to stitch well, we will show you the adjusted version on the mockup before anything is made.

Fabric pairing for embroidery

Embroidery needs fabric with enough structure to carry the stitches without puckering. Polos in cotton, matty or polycot are the classic choice, and their knit holds a chest logo beautifully. Corporate shirts in cotton or polycot give a clean, flat surface for a pocket area logo. Caps in cotton or polycot have firm fronts that keep the stitched logo standing proud.

Hoodies in sinker or cotton fleece and varsity jackets take larger embroidered logos well, which suits leadership gifts and team jackets. Very thin or very stretchy fabrics are less suited to dense embroidery, and we will advise you if your choice needs a lighter design. Tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

How long embroidery lasts, and wash care

Embroidery is the most durable decoration we offer. Thread does not crack, peel or fade the way some inks can, so an embroidered polo often looks good long after the fabric itself has softened.

A little care keeps it looking crisp:

  • Wash inside out to protect the stitches from rubbing
  • Use cold or lukewarm water and a mild detergent
  • Avoid wringing hard, which can pull the stitches
  • Iron on the reverse side with a cloth between the iron and the logo
  • For caps, spot clean or hand wash gently and air dry
