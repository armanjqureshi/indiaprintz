How DTF printing in Bandra works, step by step

DTF stands for direct to film. The name gives away the secret: your design goes onto a film first, and then from the film onto the garment. Here is the journey in plain words.

  1. Your artwork is printed onto a clear film. A special printer lays down the full colour design in pigment ink, mirrored, so it reads correctly once it is flipped onto the shirt.
  2. A white layer goes on behind the colours. This white base is what keeps reds, yellows and skin tones bright, even when the garment is black, navy or bottle green.
  3. An adhesive powder is added and cured. A fine powder sticks to the wet ink only, and gentle heat melts it into a glue layer that will later bond with the fabric.
  4. The film is heat pressed onto your garment. Heat and pressure fuse the print to the fabric at the exact spot marked on your approved mockup.
  5. The film is peeled away. What stays behind is your design, soft and flexible, sitting on the surface of the fabric.

Because no screens or stencils are made, a design with 2 colours and a design with 200 colours cost the same to print. That single fact explains why DTF is the favourite for small runs and detailed artwork. When DTF is the right pick, and when it is not

We use every method at our unit, so we have no reason to push one over another. DTF is wonderful for some jobs and simply the wrong tool for others. Here is how we think about it.

Choose DTF when:

  • Your quantity is small, from 2 to around 50 pieces.
  • The design has many colours, gradients, shadows or a photograph.
  • Each shirt needs a different name, number or role.
  • You are printing on dark fabric and want colours to stay vivid.
  • You want to mix fabrics in one order, say cotton tees and polyester shorts.

Choose something else when:

  • You are printing 100 or more tees with a simple 1 to 3 colour logo. Screen printing becomes cheaper per piece on larger runs, and our page on screen printing in Bandra explains how it works for big orders.
  • You want a stitched logo on polos, shirts or caps for a BKC team. Thread has a texture and premium feel that print cannot copy, and embroidery in Bandra is the better route there.
  • You want an all-over design on a polyester sports kit. Sublimation dyes the design into the fabric itself and suits that job better.

Many orders mix methods happily. A label might use DTF for a detailed back print and embroidery for a small chest logo on the same hoodie. If you are torn between the two most common choices, our side by side guide on DTF vs screen printing walks through the trade-offs with real numbers.

Artwork tips that make DTF prints shine

DTF reproduces what you send very faithfully, which is both its strength and a small warning. A sharp file gives a sharp print, and a blurry screenshot gives a blurry one. These tips help.

  • Send high resolution. A PNG or PDF at print size, ideally 300 dpi, is perfect. Vector files (AI, PDF, SVG) are even better for logos and text.
  • Use a transparent background. If your PNG has a white box behind the artwork, that box will print too. Remove it before sending, or tell us and we will check it with you on the mockup.
  • Watch very thin lines. Hairline strokes and tiny text below about 6 point can lose detail. Thicken them slightly for a crisp result.
  • Think about the edges of photos. A photo with a hard rectangular edge looks like a sticker. A soft fade or a cut-out shape often sits more naturally on fabric.
  • Keep names in a list. For name and number orders, send a simple spreadsheet or message with each name, number and size together, so nothing gets mixed up.
  • Check colours on the mockup. Screens show colour with light, fabric shows it with ink. Neon and very pale pastels shift the most, so review them carefully.

Fabric pairing for DTF

DTF works on most fabrics, which is one of its biggest advantages for Bandra orders that mix garments. Even so, some fabrics give a nicer result than others.

Cotton and bio-wash cotton are the classic choice for cafe tees and creator merch. They feel soft against the skin in Mumbai humidity, and the print sits neatly on a smooth surface. Bio-wash in particular has a cleaner finish that suits merch people plan to wear out.

Sinker and polycot are sturdy everyday knits. Polycot keeps its shape and colour through regular washing, which helps staff tees that go through the machine twice a week.

Polyester takes DTF well too, which makes it handy for gym tees and event crew shirts that need to dry quickly. For all-over designs on light polyester, sublimation is usually the better partner.

Fleece-backed hoodie fabric holds DTF prints nicely, as long as the print area is flat and away from thick seams and pocket edges.

Durability and wash care for DTF prints

A well pressed DTF print is flexible and holds up to regular wear and washing. The way the garment is washed makes a real difference to how long it looks fresh. Share these points with your team or customers.

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Wash in cold or lukewarm water on a gentle cycle.
  • Skip bleach and harsh stain removers on the printed area.
  • Dry in the shade. Strong sun and hot tumble drying are hard on any print.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron inside out, or place a cloth over the design.
  • Avoid dry cleaning for printed tees.

Labels selling DTF merch often print these care points on a small card in the packaging. It is a thoughtful touch that keeps customers happy with the shirt for longer.

How much does DTF printing cost in Bandra?

DTF printing starts from ₹35 per print position, added to the garment price. A round neck tee with one DTF print starts at ₹155 per piece. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp after we see your design and quantity.

Can I get just 2 DTF printed t-shirts in Bandra?

Yes. A full colour custom printed t-shirt with one design can be ordered from just 2 pieces, starting at ₹200 per piece plus printing. For example, (200 + 35 x 1) x 2 = ₹470 at starting prices.

Can DTF print a photograph on a black t-shirt?

Yes. DTF lays a white base under the colours, so photos and bright artwork stay vivid on black, navy and other dark fabrics. Send the highest resolution version of the photo you have.

Is DTF good for printing different names on each shirt?

It is ideal for that. There is no setup per design, so each piece can carry its own name or number. Send the names, numbers and sizes together in one list.

Does DTF printing work on hoodies and tote bags?

Yes. DTF works on most fabrics, including hoodie fleece and canvas totes. We keep the print on a flat area away from thick seams and pockets for a clean finish.

Will a DTF print crack after washing?

A properly pressed DTF print is flexible and handles regular washing well. Wash inside out in cold or lukewarm water, dry in the shade and never iron directly on the print to keep it looking fresh.

Is DTF or screen printing better for 30 cafe staff tees in Bandra?

For 30 tees with a detailed or multi-colour logo, DTF is usually the practical choice. If your logo is 1 to 3 flat colours and your order grows towards 100 pieces, screen printing often works out cheaper per piece.
