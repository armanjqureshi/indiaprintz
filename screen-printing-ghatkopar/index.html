How screen printing works, step by step

Screen printing is a stencil method. Ink is pushed through a fine mesh onto the fabric, one colour at a time, and the stencil decides where the ink lands. Here is the journey your design takes.

  1. Colours are separated. Your artwork is split into its individual colours. A design with a red logo and white text becomes two separate layers.
  2. A screen is made for each colour. A fine mesh is stretched on a frame and coated with a light sensitive emulsion. Your design is exposed onto it, and the unexposed areas wash away to leave an open stencil.
  3. The garment is laid flat. Each tee is placed on a printing board so it sits in exactly the same spot every time.
  4. Ink goes through the mesh. A squeegee pulls ink across the screen, pressing it through the open stencil and onto the fabric.
  5. Colours are lined up. For multi colour designs, each screen is printed in turn and carefully aligned with the last.
  6. The print is cured. Heat sets the ink so it bonds with the fibres and survives washing.

The screens take effort to prepare, and that is the whole secret of the pricing. The setup is shared across your entire run, so the more pieces you print, the cheaper each one becomes. Our main page on screen printing in Mumbai has more background on the method.

Screen printing or another method: how to decide

A quick way to decide is to look at two things: how many colours your design has, and how many pieces you need. Screen printing is the clear winner when:

  • Your design uses 1 to 3 flat colours, without photos or gradients.
  • You are printing roughly 50 pieces or more of the same design.
  • The garment is cotton or a cotton blend.
  • You want a soft, durable print that feels part of the tee.

If your artwork is full colour, has a photo, or needs a different name on every back, DTF printing in Ghatkopar handles it with no setup and no colour limit. For a premium chest logo on office polos or caps, embroidery in Ghatkopar gives a stitched finish that looks smart at a reception desk. Many groups mix methods too, for example a screen printed front with DTF names on the back.

Artwork tips that make screen prints look sharp

Screen printing rewards simple, confident artwork. A few habits will make your design print cleanly and keep the price low.

  • Count your colours. Every colour needs its own screen. Two colours print cleaner and faster than five, and many of the best festival designs use just one.
  • Send vector files. AI, PDF, EPS or SVG files keep edges crisp at any size. A blurry JPG from a WhatsApp forward will need redrawing first.
  • Share exact colour codes. If your mandal or company has a set colour, send the Pantone or hex code so we can match it closely.
  • Skip gradients and photos. Smooth fades and photographs are not what screen printing is built for. Flat, bold shapes look far better.
  • Keep text readable from a distance. On a Dahi Handi ground or a garba lane, people read your tee from metres away. Big letters and thick strokes win.
  • Keep the design the same on every piece. If names or numbers change from tee to tee, that part should be printed with DTF instead.

Best fabrics for screen printing

Screen printing loves natural fibres. Cotton soaks up the ink and holds it, which gives that soft, sharp finish people remember. Polycot and sinker blends take it very well too, which is why society and volunteer tees are usually ordered in these.

Bio-wash cotton feels extra smooth and works nicely for company tees that people will actually wear again after the event. For polyester sports tees, ask us first, since sublimation or DTF often gives a better result on slippery synthetic fabric. If you want a specific weight, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Colour matters as well. Dark ink on a light tee is the simplest and most affordable combination. Light ink on a dark tee looks striking and works well, and the mockup will show you exactly how it comes out before anything is printed.

How long screen prints last, and how to care for them

A properly cured screen print is one of the toughest decorations you can put on a tee. The ink bonds with the fibres, so it handles rain at a Dahi Handi, sweat during visarjan and many trips through the washing machine.

  • Wash inside out to protect the surface of the print.
  • Use cold or lukewarm water and a mild detergent.
  • Keep bleach well away from printed areas.
  • Dry in the shade, since harsh sun can fade both fabric and ink over time.
  • Iron on the reverse side, never directly on the print.

Follow these simple steps and your mandal tee will still look good when you pull it out of the cupboard next festival season.

How much does screen printing cost in Ghatkopar?

Screen printing starts at ₹15 per print position, per piece, on top of the garment. With round neck tees from ₹120, a front print starts at ₹135 per tee. The final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp after we see your design.

Is screen printing cheaper than DTF for 100 tees?

For a design with 1 to 3 flat colours, yes, screen printing is usually cheaper at 100 pieces. It starts at ₹15 per position compared with ₹35 for DTF. For full colour artwork or a different name on each tee, DTF is the better fit.

What is the minimum order for screen printing?

You can start from 5 pieces on most garments. That said, screen printing gives the best value from around 50 pieces upwards, because the screen setup is shared across the whole run.

Can screen printing print photos or gradients?

Screen printing is best for flat, solid colours. Photos and smooth gradients come out far better with DTF printing. If your design mixes both, we can suggest a combination.

How many colours can I use in a screen printed design?

We recommend 1 to 3 flat colours for the cleanest and most affordable result. Each colour needs its own screen, so simpler designs print faster and sharper.

Can you screen print t-shirts for our Dahi Handi pathak in Ghatkopar?

Yes. Pathaks usually choose cotton round neck tees with a bold one or two colour print on the front and back. Send your logo and size list early so we can give you a dispatch date that works for the festival.

Will screen printed tees survive rain and many washes?

A properly cured screen print bonds with the fabric and handles rain, sweat and regular washing very well. Wash inside out in cold water and avoid bleach to keep it looking fresh.
