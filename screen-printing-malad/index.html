How screen printing works, step by step

The process has a few more steps than a digital method, and each one is the reason the result looks so clean. This is what happens to your design.

  1. Colour separation. Your artwork is split into its individual colours. A two colour logo becomes two separate layers.
  2. Making the screens. Each colour gets its own mesh screen, coated with a light sensitive emulsion and exposed so that only the design area lets ink through.
  3. Lining up. The screens are aligned on the press so every colour lands in exactly the right place on every garment.
  4. Printing. Ink is placed on the screen and pulled across with a squeegee, pressing it through the mesh onto the fabric. This repeats for each colour.
  5. Curing. The printed garments pass through heat, which sets the ink so it bonds with the fibres and survives washing.

The setup in steps 1 to 3 is the same whether you print 50 tees or 500. That is why the cost per piece drops so nicely as your quantity grows. For the full picture beyond Malad, see our guide to screen printing in Mumbai.

When to choose screen printing, and when not to

We always recommend the method that suits your design and quantity, even if it is not the one you first had in mind. Here is a quick way to decide.

Screen printing is the right pick when

  • You need about 50 or more pieces with the same design
  • The artwork uses 1 to 3 flat, solid colours
  • The garment is cotton, polycot, sinker or bio-wash
  • You want a bold, opaque print that stands out from a distance
  • You expect to reorder the same design later

Another method is better when

If your design is a photo, a gradient or a full colour illustration, or if each piece needs a different name, DTF printing in Malad handles it with no setup. For all-over patterns on polyester jerseys, sublimation printing in Malad is the natural fit. And for a stitched logo on a polo or cap, embroidery gives a more premium finish.

To see the difference in money, take 100 round neck tees with a logo on the front only. With screen printing it is (120 + 15 x 1) x 100 = ₹13,500. With DTF it is (120 + 35 x 1) x 100 = ₹15,500. On a run that size with a simple logo, screen printing saves you ₹2,000.

Artwork tips that make screen prints look sharp

Good screen printing starts with artwork designed for it. A few simple choices make a big difference.

  • Keep to 1 to 3 colours. Each colour needs its own screen, so fewer colours keep things simple and clean.
  • Use solid colours, not gradients. Soft fades and photo effects are hard to reproduce on screens. Flat shapes and bold text work best.
  • Send a vector file if you can. AI, PDF, EPS or SVG files give the cleanest edges. A large, sharp PNG also works.
  • Mention exact colours. If your brand has a specific red or blue, share the colour code so we can match it closely.
  • Think about contrast. Light ink on dark tees and dark ink on light tees read best from far away, which matters in a procession or on a ground.
  • Avoid hairline details. Very thin lines and tiny text can fill in. Keep text at a readable size.

Do not worry about separating colours yourself. Send the design and we will prepare it for screens and show you a mockup.

Fabric pairing for screen printing

Screen printing ink bonds beautifully with natural fibres. Cotton is the classic choice, giving a vivid print and a soft, breathable tee for long festival days. Polycot blends keep their shape and resist wrinkles, which helps office event tees that get worn again afterwards. Sinker and bio-wash both take screen prints well and feel lovely against the skin.

Polyester can be screen printed, but it is not where the method is happiest. For fast dry sports kits, we will usually point you to sublimation or DTF instead. If weight matters to you, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

How long screen prints last, and how to care for them

A properly cured screen print is one of the toughest prints there is. The ink locks into the fibres, so the design stays bold through many washes, which is why society and mandal tees often come out again for the following year's events.

To keep it at its best:

  • Turn the tee inside out before washing.
  • Use cold water and a gentle detergent.
  • Skip bleach and strong stain removers on the printed area.
  • Line dry instead of tumble drying.
  • Iron on the reverse side only.

Over a long life the print may soften and fade a little, which many people actually like. It will not peel away the way a cheap transfer can.

How much does screen printing cost in Malad?

Is screen printing cheaper than DTF for 100 tees?

For a simple logo in 1 to 3 colours, yes. 100 round neck tees with a front print start at ₹13,500 with screen printing and ₹15,500 with DTF.

What is the minimum order for screen printing in Malad?

How many colours can a screen print have?

We recommend 1 to 3 flat colours for the cleanest result and the best price. Designs with photos or gradients are better suited to DTF.
