How logo embroidery works, step by step

Embroidery uses a computer controlled embroidery machine to sew your logo into the fabric with coloured thread. It looks simple when finished, but there is real craft behind every piece. Here is the journey once you approve your mockup:

  1. Digitising. Your logo is converted into a stitch file, which is a detailed plan telling the machine where every stitch goes, in what direction and in which colour.
  2. Choosing threads. Thread colours are matched to your logo as closely as possible.
  3. Hooping. The garment is held tight in a hoop, with a stabilising backing underneath so the fabric does not pucker while it is stitched.
  4. Stitching. The machine sews the design colour by colour, building up the logo layer by layer.
  5. Finishing. Loose threads are trimmed, the extra backing is cleared away, and each piece is checked before packing.

Digitising is the step that makes or breaks embroidery. A logo that has been planned well looks smooth and full, with clean edges and readable lettering. Our main page on embroidery in Mumbai has more on the process across the city.

When embroidery is the best choice, and when printing wins

Embroidery is premium and long lasting, but it is not right for every design. Here is how to decide.

Choose embroidery when:

  • The logo will be worn every day, on uniforms that are washed often.
  • You want a premium, textured look on polos, shirts, caps or jackets.
  • Your logo is reasonably simple, with bold shapes and clear lettering.
  • The logo sits in a small area, such as the chest, sleeve or cap front.

Choose printing when:

  • Your logo has a photo, a gradient or very fine detail. Very fine text and photos cannot be embroidered, so DTF printing in Mira Road is the better route for those.
  • You need a large design across the full back of a tee. A big embroidered area becomes heavy and stiff on a light garment.
  • You are ordering many event tees on a tight budget with a simple design. Screen printing in Mira Road is far more economical for that.

Many businesses combine the two beautifully: an embroidered logo on the chest for a premium look, and a printed slogan or website on the back.

Getting your logo ready for embroidery

The better your logo is prepared, the better it looks in thread. These tips help you get the cleanest result.

  • Send the cleanest file you have. A vector file such as PDF or AI is ideal. A clear, high resolution PNG also works well for digitising.
  • Keep it simple. Bold shapes, solid colours and clean outlines stitch beautifully. Tiny details, thin lines and shading tend to disappear or look crowded.
  • Watch the small text. Taglines and phone numbers in tiny letters cannot be stitched cleanly. Keep the brand name bold and move the fine print to a printed area if you need it.
  • Limit thread colours. A logo with a handful of solid colours looks crisp. If yours has many shades, we suggest a simplified version that still feels like your brand.
  • Tell us the placement and size. Left chest, right chest, sleeve, cap front or back of a jacket, each changes how the logo is planned.
  • Share your brand colours. A colour code or a photo of your signage helps us match the thread closely.

If your logo is very detailed, do not worry. We will happily suggest a version that keeps its spirit and looks great in thread, and you see it on the mockup before anything is stitched.

Garments and fabrics that suit embroidery

Embroidery needs a fabric that can hold stitches without puckering, which is why it is at home on polos, shirts, caps and jackets. These garments have enough body to carry a stitched logo neatly.

For polos, matty fabric is a wonderful match. Its textured weave holds stitches well, and it hides creases through a long shift at a showroom or clinic. Cotton and polycot polos are also popular. For shirts, polycot keeps its shape and colour through daily wear, which suits Mira Road's humid weather and frequent washing.

Light, thin tees are not ideal for embroidery, especially for large designs, because the fabric can pull around the stitches. For tees, printing usually gives a better result. If you prefer a particular fabric weight, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Caring for embroidered uniforms

Embroidered logos are among the most durable decorations you can put on a garment. The thread is sewn right into the fabric, so there is nothing to crack or peel. A little care keeps it looking crisp.

  • Wash inside out, in cold or lukewarm water.
  • Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach, which can fade thread colours.
  • Do not scrub the embroidered area with a brush.
  • Iron from the reverse side, with a towel under the logo so the stitches keep their raised texture.
  • Dry flat or on a hanger in the shade, rather than wringing the garment hard.

Follow these steps, and your team's polos will look as smart on the hundredth day as on the first.
