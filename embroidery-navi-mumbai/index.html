How embroidery works, from logo to stitched finish

Embroidery builds your design out of thread, stitch by stitch. It takes a little preparation, and that preparation is what makes a logo look sharp.

  1. Digitising. Your logo is converted into a stitch file. This tells the machine which stitch type to use in each area, in what direction and in what order.
  2. Choosing threads. Thread colours are matched to your logo colours as closely as possible.
  3. Hooping. The garment is stretched in a frame with a stabiliser behind it, so the fabric stays firm while it is stitched.
  4. Stitching. The machine sews the design, colour by colour, building up satin stitches for text and borders and fill stitches for larger areas.
  5. Finishing. Loose threads are trimmed, the backing is cleaned up and the garment is checked before packing.

Since the logo is made of thread, it has a gentle raised texture you can feel. That texture is a big part of why embroidery reads as premium.

When to choose embroidery, and when print is the better pick

Embroidery is not the answer for every design. Knowing its strengths helps you get a better result for your money.

Choose embroidery when

  • You want a premium, long lasting finish for a logo or short text.
  • The garment is a polo, shirt, cap, jacket or hoodie.
  • The design is simple, with bold shapes and a limited number of colours.
  • The clothing will be worn and washed often, as with daily uniforms.
  • You want a small chest or sleeve logo rather than a large back design.

Pick a printed method when

Very fine text and photos cannot be embroidered. If your design is a photograph, a gradient or a detailed illustration, DTF printing in Navi Mumbai reproduces it in full colour on most fabrics. For large back designs on 100 or more cotton tees in 1 to 3 colours, screen printing is far more economical. For polyester sports jerseys with all-over designs, sublimation is the right method.

Many teams combine methods: an embroidered logo on the chest for the premium look, and a printed slogan or event name on the back. We quote each position separately, so it stays clear.

Preparing your logo for embroidery

A logo that looks great on screen may need small changes to stitch beautifully. These tips save time and give you a cleaner result.

  • Send vector files if possible. AI, EPS, PDF or SVG files make digitising faster and more accurate. A clear, high resolution PNG also works.
  • Keep text bold and not too small. Very fine text cannot be embroidered cleanly. As a guide, letters should be at least about 5 mm tall on a chest logo, and thicker fonts stitch better than thin ones.
  • Simplify fine details. Hairline strokes, tiny dots and soft shadows do not translate to thread. A slightly simplified version of your logo often looks better stitched.
  • Limit colours. Each colour is a separate thread. Logos with a handful of solid colours look neat and crisp.
  • Mention the size and placement. A left chest logo is usually 3 to 4 inches wide, while a cap front panel is smaller. Tell us where each logo goes.
  • Share brand colour codes. We match thread colours as closely as possible and show them in the mockup.

Before production, we share a mockup of your logo on the garment so you can approve size, placement and colours. Nothing is stitched until you are happy.

Fabric pairing for embroidery

Embroidery needs fabric with enough body to hold stitches firmly. These pairings work especially well.

  • Matty polos have a textured weave that holds embroidery neatly and keeps its shape, making them the classic corporate choice.
  • Polycot shirts and polos wrinkle less and keep the stitched area flat through frequent washing.
  • Cotton caps give a firm front panel for a crisp logo.
  • Fleece hoodies and zipper hoodies handle chest and sleeve embroidery well.
  • Varsity and track jackets provide a sturdy base for bold lettering and badges.

Very thin or stretchy fabrics can pucker around stitching, so we avoid them for embroidery. If you want a specific GSM for your polos, tell us the GSM you want and we will confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for embroidered garments

Embroidery is one of the most durable ways to brand clothing. The thread does not crack or peel, and good care keeps it looking new.

  • Wash inside out to protect the stitches from rubbing.
  • Use cold or lukewarm water and a mild detergent, and avoid bleach.
  • Do not scrub the embroidered area with a brush.
  • Iron on the reverse side, ideally with a towel underneath, so the stitches keep their raised look.
  • Dry in the shade and avoid wringing the embroidered area hard.
  • For caps, spot clean or hand wash gently, then let them air dry in shape.

With these simple habits, embroidered polos and shirts stay sharp through months of daily wear.
