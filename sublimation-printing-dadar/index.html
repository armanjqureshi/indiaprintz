What sublimation printing is and how it works

Sublimation uses heat to turn special dyes into a gas that bonds with polyester fibres. The result is a design that becomes part of the fabric instead of a layer on top. Here is how a jersey goes from screen to field.

  1. Design. Your kit design is laid out on a template for each panel, front, back and sleeves, with names, numbers and sponsor logos placed exactly.
  2. Printing on paper. The design is printed in mirror image with sublimation inks onto transfer paper.
  3. Pressing. The paper is placed on white or light polyester fabric and pressed under high heat.
  4. Bonding. The heat turns the ink into a gas that sinks into the polyester fibres and locks in as it cools.
  5. Stitching and finishing. The printed fabric is cut and stitched into the finished jersey, shorts or jacket, then checked and packed.

Since nothing sits on top of the fabric, the jersey feels exactly like plain polyester. It stays soft, light and breathable, which matters a lot on a sticky Mumbai afternoon.

Why sublimation only works on polyester

The dye bonds with polyester fibres specifically. Cotton does not hold it, so on a cotton tee the colour would simply wash out. That is why every sublimated product we make is in polyester.

Why the fabric must be white or light

Sublimation inks are slightly see-through, and there is no white ink in the process. The fabric itself provides the white, so the base must be white or light for the colours to show true. If you want a black or navy jersey, we print the dark colour as part of the design on white polyester.

When to choose sublimation, and when another method is better

Sublimation is the clear winner when:

  • the garment is polyester and white or light based,
  • you want all-over designs, stripes, gradients or photos,
  • every piece needs its own name and number,
  • the kit needs to stay light, breathable and crack-free through hard play.

If your group prefers cotton tees, sublimation will not work, and the choice moves to other methods. For large runs of cotton tees with a simple design, screen printing in Dadar is usually the most economical. For full colour on cotton or on dark garments, DTF printing in Dadar is the better fit, since it works on most fabrics. And a club crest stitched on a track jacket is a job for embroidery.

Designing for sublimation: artwork tips

Sublimation gives you a lot of creative freedom. A few pointers help you use it well.

  • Go bold with colour. Gradients, patterns, textures and even photos all print well. This is the method for the kit you have been sketching in your notebook.
  • Remember there is no white ink. Any white in your design is simply the fabric showing through, so plan white areas with that in mind.
  • Send layered or vector files. AI, PDF or high resolution PNG files work best. Keep sponsor logos as separate vector files so they stay sharp.
  • Make names and numbers readable from a distance. Pick a clear, bold font and make sure it contrasts strongly with the colour behind it. A white number on a pale yellow panel is hard to read from the boundary.
  • Keep key elements away from seams. Logos and numbers placed right on a side seam or under the arm can look broken. We check placement on the mockup.
  • Send the player list as typed text. Name, number and size for each player, in a message or spreadsheet, avoids spelling mistakes.

Screen colours and fabric colours differ slightly, so look closely at the mockup. If your club has fixed colours, share the codes and we will match them as closely as the method allows.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Since the colour lives inside the fibres, sublimated designs do not crack, peel or flake. They hold their brightness very well through regular washing, which is why clubs often get more than one season out of a kit. Polyester just needs a little care.

  • Wash the kit soon after each match or run so sweat does not sit in the fabric.
  • Use cold water and a mild detergent, and skip bleach and fabric softener.
  • Turn jerseys inside out before washing.
  • Dry in the shade and avoid wringing hard.
  • Avoid high heat. Do not iron directly on the design and avoid hot tumble drying, since strong heat can affect polyester.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Dadar?

Sublimation starts at ₹25 per print position, per piece, on top of the garment price. A polyester sports jersey from ₹200 printed front and back starts at ₹250 a piece. We confirm the final price on WhatsApp once we see your design and quantity.

Can you do sublimation printing on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation dyes bond only with polyester, so the design would wash out of cotton. For cotton tees we recommend DTF printing for full colour designs, or screen printing for larger runs with simple artwork.

Can I get sublimated kabaddi jerseys with names in Dadar?

Yes. Each jersey can carry its own name and number at no extra setup, because they are printed as part of the design. Send a typed list of names, numbers and sizes, and we check every one with you on the mockup.

Why does sublimation need white or light polyester?

Sublimation has no white ink, and the inks are slightly see-through, so the fabric provides the base colour. On white or light polyester the colours show bright and true. For a dark kit, we print the dark colour onto white polyester as part of the design.

Will sublimated jerseys fade after many washes?

Sublimated designs hold their colour very well because the dye is inside the fibres, not on the surface. Wash in cold water, avoid bleach and keep high heat away from the jersey. With that care, kits stay bright for a long time.

What is the minimum order for sublimated jerseys in Dadar?

Sports jerseys, shorts, polyester tees, track jackets and windcheaters all start at 5 pieces. That makes sublimation practical for a small five-a-side team as well as a full league.

Is sublimation or DTF better for a running group's tees?

If the tees are white or light polyester, sublimation is usually better because the print adds no weight and the fabric stays breathable on long runs. If the group wants cotton or dark coloured tees, DTF is the right choice. We can quote both.
