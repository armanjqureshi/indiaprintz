What screen printing is and how it works

Screen printing is one of the oldest and most reliable ways of putting a design on fabric. Ink is pushed through a fine mesh screen onto the garment, one colour at a time. Here is how it happens, from your file to the finished tee:

  1. The design is separated into colours. A two colour design becomes two separate layers, one for each ink.
  2. A screen is made for each colour. Each layer is exposed onto a mesh screen coated with a light sensitive emulsion, leaving open mesh only where that colour should print.
  3. The garment is placed on the press. It is laid flat on a board and lined up so every tee in the batch gets the print in the same spot.
  4. Ink is pulled through the screen. A squeegee drags ink across the screen, pressing it through the open mesh onto the fabric. For a second colour, the next screen is lined up and printed.
  5. The print is cured with heat. Heat sets the ink so it bonds with the fibres and survives washing.

Because each colour needs its own screen, the setup work is the same for 50 tees as it is for 500. Spread that setup over a large run and each piece becomes very economical, which is why screen printing starts at just ₹15 per print position.

When to choose screen printing and when another method wins

Screen printing is the most economical choice for the right design, but it has limits. Here is how to know.

Screen printing is the best pick when:

  • You are ordering about 50 or more pieces of the same design
  • The design uses 1 to 3 flat, solid colours
  • The garments are cotton or a cotton blend
  • You want a bold, opaque print that pops on both light and dark tees

Another method is better when:

  • The design has a photo, gradients or many colours, or each piece needs its own name. DTF printing in Vasai handles all of that with no setup, from ₹35 per position.
  • You need football or cricket jerseys with all-over patterns on white polyester. Sublimation printing in Vasai dyes the design into the fabric so it never peels.
  • You want a refined stitched logo on a polo or cap, which is where embroidery comes in.

Preparing artwork for screen printing

Screen printing loves clean, confident artwork. A few habits make your design print sharply from the first tee to the last.

  • Limit the colours. Each colour is a separate screen, so a design in 1 to 3 colours prints best and costs least. If your logo has five colours, we can suggest a simplified version.
  • Send vector files if you can. AI, PDF, SVG or EPS files give the cleanest edges. A high resolution PNG also works, and we will prepare it for screens.
  • Use solid shapes, not soft fades. Gradients and photo shading do not separate well into flat colours. Bold outlines and solid fills look fantastic.
  • Keep lines and text a sensible thickness. Very thin strokes can fill in or break up. If a line is barely visible on your screen at actual size, it is too thin.
  • Name your ink colours. Tell us the exact shades you want, for example "white and maroon on saffron", and mention the garment colour too.
  • Check the spelling twice. With 150 tees in one run, a typo is expensive to fix, so read the mandal or parish name letter by letter in the mockup.

We send a mockup of your design on the actual garment before anything is printed, so the whole committee can approve it together.

Fabric pairing for screen printed tees

Screen printing performs best on cotton and cotton rich blends. The fibres soak up the ink and hold it firmly, which gives a bright, even print with a slightly raised feel.

For mandal and feast tees, cotton or bio-wash cotton keeps volunteers comfortable through long, humid days. Polycot is a smart pick for school house tees and warehouse staff, because it shrinks less and survives frequent washing. Sinker gives a soft, everyday feel that people enjoy wearing again after the event.

Screen printing can also work on polyester and safety jacket fabric, though cotton remains its happiest home. If you are planning a heavier or lighter tee for the mandal, share the GSM you have in mind and we will check what is available.

Durability and wash care for screen prints

A properly cured screen print is one of the toughest prints you can get. The ink becomes part of the fabric surface and usually lasts for years of regular wear. To keep it looking fresh:

  • Wash inside out in cold or normal temperature water.
  • Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach on the print.
  • Dry in the shade, since strong sun can fade any garment over time.
  • Iron on the reverse side, never directly over the print.
  • Avoid wringing the printed area hard when washing by hand.

Many mandal members wear last year's tee to the next year's preparation meetings, which is the best proof a screen print can offer.

What is the minimum order for screen printing in Virar?

Most of our garments start at 5 pieces. That said, screen printing gives its lowest price per piece at about 50 or more, because the screen setup is shared across the run. For smaller quantities, DTF is often the better value.

Is screen printing cheaper than DTF for 100 tees?

For a design with 1 to 3 flat colours, yes. Screen printing starts at ₹15 per position against ₹35 for DTF, so 100 round neck tees with one front print start at ₹13,500 with screen printing and ₹15,500 with DTF. If the design has a photo or individual names, DTF is the better fit.

How many colours can be screen printed on a mandal t-shirt?

Can you screen print on dark t-shirts in Nalasopara?
