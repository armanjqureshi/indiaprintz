How sublimation printing works

Sublimation uses heat to turn special ink into a gas that bonds with polyester. It sounds like a science class, but the process is easy to picture:

  1. Your design is printed in mirror image onto transfer paper using sublimation inks.
  2. The paper is laid on white or light coloured polyester fabric, lined up with the print position.
  3. A heat press applies high heat and pressure, and the ink turns into a gas that enters the polyester fibres.
  4. As the fabric cools, the colour is locked inside the fibres, and the paper is removed.

The result has almost no feel on the skin. Run your hand over a sublimated jersey and you cannot tell where the print begins. This is also why sublimation has two firm rules. It only works on polyester, because cotton fibres cannot hold the dye, and it needs a white or light base, because the dye is see-through and cannot cover a dark fabric.

When to choose sublimation, and when another method is better

Sublimation is unbeatable for sportswear, but it has clear limits. Use this checklist to see if it fits your order.

Sublimation is the right pick when:

  • The garment is polyester with a white or light base colour
  • You want stripes, panels, patterns or colour across large areas
  • The kit will be worn for sport, running or dancing, with lots of sweat and washing
  • You need names and numbers on every jersey
  • You want a print that cannot be felt and will not crack or peel

Another method is better in a few situations. For cotton tees, sublimation simply does not work. If you need 50 or more cotton tees with a simple 1 to 3 colour design, screen printing in Borivali is the economical choice. For bright colours on dark polyester, or full colour photos on cotton, DTF printing handles it.

For a stitched crest on a team track jacket or the coach's cap, embroidery in Borivali gives a premium, textured finish that looks great alongside a sublimated kit. Many teams mix the two: sublimated jerseys for match days, and embroidered jackets and caps for travel and team photos.

Artwork tips for sublimation

Sublimation can reproduce almost any colour and detail, so the possibilities are wide. A few tips help your kit come out exactly as you imagined.

  • White in your design means no ink. Sublimation cannot print white, so any white area shows the fabric colour. On a white jersey that is perfect, but plan for it on light coloured bases.
  • Design to the edges. Stripes, gradients and patterns that run across the whole jersey are what sublimation does best. Tell us if you want an all-over design so we can quote it correctly.
  • Send high resolution files. Large patterns need large files. Vector files work best for logos and sponsor marks, and photos should be sharp at full size.
  • Send one clear player list. Write each name, number and size on a single line in one WhatsApp message or spreadsheet, spelt exactly as you want them printed.
  • Keep numbers bold. Big, thick numbers in a strong contrasting colour are easy to read from the boundary or the stands.
  • Expect slight colour shifts. Colours on fabric can look a little different from your phone screen, which is why the mockup step matters. Check it carefully.

If you have a sponsor or a society logo to include, send it separately as its own file. It keeps the logo crisp wherever it sits on the kit.

Fabric pairing for sublimation

Sublimation and polyester are a perfect match, and there is no substitute. The dye bonds only with polyester fibres, so cotton, polycot and other blends do not give a good result.

  • Polyester jerseys are light, breathable and quick drying, made for cricket, football and turf games.
  • Polyester shorts complete the kit and carry the same stripes or panels as the jersey.
  • Polyester round necks suit running clubs, cycling groups and garba tees where comfort in heat matters most.
  • Polyester polos work for coaches, umpires and team managers who want a collar with the team design.
  • Polyester track jackets keep the team warm on early mornings, with a sublimated design that matches the kit.

Remember the colour rule as well. White and light bases such as cream, pale grey or light yellow give the brightest results. For black or navy kits, choose DTF instead. For a particular fabric weight, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Because the colour lives inside the fibres, sublimated prints do not crack, peel or fade quickly. A well-made sublimated jersey can go through a full season of weekly matches and washes and still look bright in the final. That is a big reason sports teams prefer it.

A little care keeps your kit fresh:

  1. Rinse sweaty jerseys soon after the match instead of leaving them in a kit bag overnight.
  2. Wash in cold water with a mild detergent, inside out if you can.
  3. Avoid fabric softener, which can affect how polyester handles sweat.
  4. Dry in the shade, since strong sun is hard on every fabric.
  5. Do not iron directly on the design, because high heat can affect the dye. Polyester rarely needs ironing anyway.

A team kit cared for this way stays bright through a season of turf games, which keeps the squad looking good in every photo.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Borivali?

Sublimation starts at ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. For example, 15 polyester jerseys printed on the front and back start at ₹3,750. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp.

Can sublimation printing be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation only works on polyester, because cotton fibres cannot hold the dye. For cotton tees, DTF or screen printing is the right choice.

Why does sublimation need a white or light fabric?

Sublimation dye is see-through, so it cannot cover a dark base colour. White and light polyester gives the brightest, truest colours.

Can you sublimate names and numbers on box cricket jerseys in Kandivali?

Yes. Send us each player's name, number and size in one list, and we print every jersey to match. Names and numbers usually go on the back as one print position.

Will a sublimated jersey fade after many washes?

Sublimated colour is dyed into the fibres, so it does not crack or peel and holds up well through regular washing. Rinse jerseys soon after matches and wash them in cold water to keep them bright.
