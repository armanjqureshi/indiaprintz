How embroidery works, from logo to stitched badge

Embroidery uses coloured thread to stitch your logo directly into the fabric. The steps behind a neat result are simple to follow.

  1. Your logo is digitised. Digitising means converting the artwork into a stitch file that tells the needle where to go, in which direction and in what order.
  2. Thread colours are matched. We pick thread shades that sit as close as possible to your brand colours.
  3. The garment is hooped with a backing. A stabiliser goes behind the fabric so the stitching stays flat and does not pucker.
  4. The logo is stitched colour by colour. Fill stitches create solid areas and satin stitches form smooth edges and lettering.
  5. Loose threads are trimmed and the backing is cleaned up. The piece is checked before it moves on to packing.

Because every logo is stitched rather than printed, the result feels raised and rich. You can read more about the method on our main page for embroidery.

When embroidery is right, and when to print instead

Embroidery is a premium finish, but not every design was made for thread. Here is how to tell.

Choose embroidery when:

  • You want a logo that looks and feels premium on staff wear
  • The design is a clean logo, initials or a short name
  • The garment is a polo, shirt, cap, jacket or hoodie
  • The uniform will be worn and washed several times a week

Choose a print when:

  • Your design includes a photo, a colour gradient or very fine text. Thread cannot reproduce those details, so DTF printing in Mulund is the better choice.
  • You need a large design across the full back. Big stitched areas get heavy and stiff, so printing works better there.
  • You are ordering 50 or more event tees with a simple logo. For that, screen printing in Mulund keeps the cost low.

Many of our customers combine the two. An embroidered logo on the chest and a printed slogan on the back gives the best of both, and the mockup shows you exactly how it will look.

Preparing your logo for embroidery

A good stitch file starts with a good logo. Here is what helps us give you the neatest possible result.

  • Send a vector or high resolution file. PDF, AI, EPS or SVG files are ideal, and a large clear PNG also works.
  • Keep text short and not too small. Very tiny letters lose their shape in thread. If your logo has a long tagline in small type, we may suggest leaving it out of the stitched version.
  • Limit the colours. Every thread colour is a change on the machine, and logos with fewer colours look cleaner.
  • Avoid gradients and shadows. Thread is a solid colour, so we convert fades into a few clean shades or a single tone.
  • Tell us the brand colours. Share colour codes or a photo of your signboard so we can pick the closest thread shades.
  • Mention the placement. Left chest, above a pocket, centre of a cap or on a sleeve each need a slightly different size.

If your logo needs a small adjustment to stitch well, we will tell you before anything is made. You then see the result on the mockup and approve it with confidence.

Fabric pairing for embroidery

Embroidery is happiest on fabric that is thick enough to carry the stitches without puckering.

  • Matty and cotton polos are the classic choice, firm enough for a crisp chest logo.
  • Polycot shirts hold their shape and look smart at a front desk after many washes.
  • Cotton and polyester caps carry a front crest beautifully because the panel is structured.
  • Sinker and cotton hoodies take a chest logo well and feel cosy in Mulund's cooler months.
  • Varsity and track jackets offer plenty of body for initials, badges and names.

Very thin or stretchy fabrics, such as lightweight polyester sports tees, are less suited to dense stitching. For those, we usually suggest a print instead. For polos and shirts, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for embroidered garments

Embroidery is one of the longest lasting ways to put a logo on a garment. The thread is part of the fabric, so there is nothing to peel or crack. A few simple habits keep it looking sharp.

  1. Wash inside out to protect the stitches from rubbing against other clothes.
  2. Choose a gentle cycle or hand wash for polos and shirts worn daily.
  3. Skip bleach, which can dull coloured thread.
  4. Never iron directly on the embroidery. Press from the reverse side with a cloth on top.
  5. Keep hooks and zips closed in the wash so nothing snags a stitch.
  6. Dry flat or on a hanger so the logo area stays smooth.

A staff polo cared for like this keeps its crisp logo through months of daily shifts.

Can you embroider a logo on caps?

Yes, caps are one of the best garments for embroidery because the front panel is firm and structured. Caps start from ₹80 per piece, plus ₹45 for the stitched logo.

Can small text or a tagline be embroidered?

Very fine text cannot be stitched cleanly, because thread needs a little space to form each letter. We may suggest enlarging the text or leaving the tagline out, and you will see the result on the mockup first.

Is embroidery better than printing for staff uniforms?

For polos, shirts and caps worn every day, embroidery gives a premium look and holds up very well to frequent washing. For large back designs, photos or event tees in bulk, printing is usually the better choice.

Can embroidery reproduce a photo or gradient logo?

No, photos and gradients cannot be embroidered. We can simplify a gradient logo into solid thread colours, or print that design using DTF instead.

How should I wash embroidered polos?

Turn them inside out, use a gentle wash, avoid bleach and iron only from the reverse side. This keeps the stitches smooth and the colours bright.
