How embroidery works, from logo to stitched badge

Embroidery is not printed at all. Your logo is built up from thousands of tiny stitches of coloured thread, sewn directly into the garment. Here is what happens along the way.

  1. Digitising the logo. Your artwork is converted into a stitch file. This is a skilled step where each part of the logo is assigned a stitch type, a direction and a density, so the final result looks clean and lies flat.
  2. Choosing threads. Thread colours are matched to your brand colours as closely as possible.
  3. Hooping. The garment is stretched in a frame called a hoop, with a backing layer behind the fabric to keep it stable while the needle works.
  4. Stitching. The embroidery machine sews the design colour by colour, following the stitch file exactly.
  5. Finishing. Loose threads are trimmed, extra backing is removed, and the piece is checked before packing.

Because digitising happens once per design, embroidery is a smart choice for logos you will use again and again, across polos, shirts, caps and jackets.

When to choose embroidery, and when another method fits better

Embroidery is gorgeous, but it has limits, and we would rather you know them before you order.

Embroidery is the right choice when:

  • You want a premium, textured logo on polos, shirts, caps or jackets
  • The logo is clean and simple, with a handful of solid colours
  • The uniform will be washed often and needs to keep looking new
  • You want a look that feels formal enough for clients and guests

Another method is better when:

  • Your design includes a photo, a gradient or very fine text. These cannot be embroidered well, so DTF printing in Kurla will reproduce them far more faithfully.
  • You need a large, low cost batch of event tees with a bold logo. Screen printing in Kurla keeps the cost per piece down on runs of about 50 and above.
  • The garment is a lightweight polyester sports jersey. Sublimation suits that fabric far better than thread.

Many teams mix methods in one order, such as embroidered polos for staff and screen printed tees for an event, and that works beautifully.

Preparing your logo for embroidery

A logo that looks great on a website does not always translate directly into thread. These tips help your design stitch cleanly.

  • Simplify where you can. Embroidery loves bold shapes and clear outlines. Tiny details, thin shadows and fine textures tend to blur together.
  • Watch the small text. Very fine text cannot be embroidered clearly. If your logo has a tagline in small letters, consider dropping it or making it larger.
  • Limit the colours. A handful of solid thread colours gives a cleaner result than trying to mimic many shades.
  • Avoid photos and gradients. They simply do not work in thread. Keep those designs for DTF.
  • Send a vector file. A PDF, AI, EPS or SVG file makes digitising more accurate. A high resolution PNG also works.
  • Share your brand colours. Tell us the exact colours you use, and we will match thread as closely as we can and show you on the mockup.
  • Think about size. A left chest logo is usually modest in size. If yours is very detailed, a slightly larger placement on the back of a jacket may show it off better.

If any part of your logo will not stitch well, we will suggest a gentle adjustment before anything goes on fabric.

Fabric pairing for embroidery

Embroidery needs a fabric that can support the stitches without puckering. That is why polos, corporate shirts, caps, hoodies and jackets are its natural partners.

Cotton and matty polos give a classic, office ready base. Polycot shirts and polos hold their shape through frequent washing, which suits uniforms. Sinker hoodies and zipper hoodies take a chest logo beautifully, and varsity and track jackets are sturdy enough for larger designs on the back. Caps in cotton or polycot have a firm front panel that shows off stitching well.

Very thin, lightweight tees are not ideal for heavy embroidery, since the fabric can gather around the stitches. For those, a print usually looks better. If you have a preferred fabric weight, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

How long embroidery lasts, and how to care for it

Embroidery is one of the most durable ways to brand clothing. Thread does not crack or peel, and good quality thread keeps its colour through regular washing. That is a big reason why uniforms washed several times a week are so often embroidered.

A little care keeps it looking its best:

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Wash in cold or normal water with a mild detergent.
  • Avoid bleach, which can affect thread colours.
  • Do not wring the embroidered area tightly.
  • Dry in the shade.
  • Iron from the reverse side, ideally with a cloth between the iron and the garment.
  • Keep embroidered pieces away from rough velcro in the wash, which can catch threads.
