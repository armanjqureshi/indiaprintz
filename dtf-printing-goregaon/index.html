How DTF printing works, step by step

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the garment, the design is first printed onto a clear film and then transferred to the fabric with heat. It sounds technical, but the steps are easy to follow:

  1. The artwork is printed onto film. Your design is printed in full colour onto a clear PET film, with a layer of white ink laid behind the colours. That white layer is the reason DTF stays bright on black, navy and maroon garments.
  2. Adhesive powder goes on. While the ink is still wet, a fine powder is spread over the film. It sticks only to the printed areas and the rest is shaken off.
  3. The film is cured. Gentle heat melts the powder into a thin, even layer of glue on the back of the print.
  4. The print is pressed onto the garment. The film is placed on the tee, hoodie or bag and pressed with heat and pressure, which bonds the print to the fibres.
  5. The film is peeled away. What remains is your design on the fabric, with sharp edges and every colour intact.

Because nothing has to be built for each colour, there is no setup step at all. A design with two colours and a design with two hundred cost the same to print. That is also why DTF is so friendly to small orders and to orders where every piece is slightly different.

When to choose DTF, and when another method suits better

DTF is a brilliant all-rounder, but it is not the answer to every order. Here is how we usually think it through with customers.

DTF is the right call when:

  • You need fewer than about 50 pieces of a design
  • The artwork has photos, gradients, shadows or many colours
  • Each piece carries a different name, number, role or department
  • You want bright, full colour artwork on dark garments
  • One order mixes different items, such as tees, hoodies and totes, with the same design

Another method may serve you better when:

  • You are ordering 100 or more tees with a simple one or two colour logo. Here screen printing in Goregaon brings the price per piece down. For 100 round neck tees printed front and back, screen printing starts at (120 + 15 x 2) x 100 = ₹15,000, while DTF starts at (120 + 35 x 2) x 100 = ₹19,000.
  • You want a premium stitched logo on polos, shirts, caps or jackets for an office front desk. Embroidery in Goregaon gives that textured, finished look.
  • You need all-over designs on white polyester sports jerseys. Sublimation dyes the design into the fabric and suits kits better.

If you are still unsure, tell us your quantity and send the artwork. We will suggest the method that gives you the best result for your budget.

Artwork tips for sharp DTF prints

DTF will print almost anything, which makes good artwork even more important. A few small habits make a big difference:

  • Send the best file you have. Vector files such as AI, PDF or SVG are ideal. For images, a PNG at 300 dpi at the actual print size keeps edges clean.
  • Use a transparent background. If your PNG has a white or coloured box behind the design, that box gets printed too. A transparent background means only the artwork lands on the fabric.
  • Go easy on soft glows and faded shadows. DTF prints solid shapes beautifully, but very faint, semi-transparent effects can look like a halo on the garment. We point these out on the mockup so you can adjust.
  • Keep fine lines and tiny text readable. Hairline strokes and very small lettering can lose detail. If you would struggle to read it on your phone screen at arm's length, it is worth making a little bigger.
  • Put names in a sheet. For name or department backs, send a simple sheet with name, back text and size in separate columns, and double check the spellings before you send it.
  • Share brand colour references. Screens show colours differently, so a Pantone number or a photo of an existing printed item helps us match your colours closely.

Fabrics that pair well with DTF

One of the nicest things about DTF is that it is not fussy about fabric. It sits happily on cotton, polyester, sinker, polycot, bio-wash and matty, as well as canvas totes and polyester backpacks. That makes it ideal when one order mixes cotton crew tees with polyester bags.

The print forms a thin, flexible layer on the surface of the fabric. A chest logo is barely noticeable, while a very large solid back print feels a little heavier. For summer tees with big artwork, designs with some open space between elements feel lighter and breathe better.

For long, humid days on a shoot, cotton and bio-wash are the most comfortable bases. For outdoor events and sport, polyester dries faster. Caring for DTF printed clothes

A well-made DTF print stays bright and flexible through many washes, and a little care keeps it that way:

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Use cold or lukewarm water and a mild detergent, and skip the bleach.
  • Dry in the shade on a line rather than in a hot dryer.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron the garment inside out, or place a cotton cloth over the design.
  • Avoid wringing the printed area hard when the tee is wet.

Crews who wash their tees after every shoot week will find these habits keep the print looking new for a long time.

Yes. Our full colour custom printed t-shirt with one design has a minimum of only 2 pieces, which is perfect for gifts or a sample. Most other garments start from 5 pieces.

Can every crew t-shirt have a different name or department on the back?

Yes. DTF has no setup, so each back can carry a different name, role or department at the same price per position. Send a sheet with each person's back text and size.

Does DTF printing work on black t-shirts?

It works very well. A white layer is printed behind the colours, so the design stays bright on black, navy and other dark shades.

Is DTF better than screen printing for 30 fest t-shirts?

For 30 tees with a colourful design, DTF is usually the better choice because there is no setup and it reproduces gradients and photos. Screen printing becomes more economical from about 50 pieces with 1 to 3 flat colours.

Will a DTF print crack or peel after washing?

A good DTF print is flexible and holds up well through regular washing. Wash inside out in cold water, dry in the shade and avoid ironing directly on the print to keep it looking fresh.

Can you DTF print a photo on a hoodie?

Yes. Photos print cleanly with DTF on pullover and zipper hoodies, and hoodies start at ₹350 before printing. Send the highest resolution photo you have for the best result.
