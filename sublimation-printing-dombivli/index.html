How sublimation printing works

Sublimation sounds scientific, and it is, but the steps are easy to follow. The key idea is that the ink becomes part of the fabric rather than sitting on it.

  1. Your design is printed onto transfer paper. Special sublimation inks print your artwork, mirrored, onto a sheet of paper.
  2. The paper is placed on the polyester fabric. It lines up with the panel or garment where the design should go.
  3. Heat and pressure are applied. Under heat, the solid ink turns straight into a gas without becoming liquid first.
  4. The gas bonds into the polyester fibres. As it cools, the colour is locked inside the fibres themselves.
  5. The paper is removed. What remains is a vivid design that you cannot feel with your fingers.

Since the ink becomes part of the fibre, there is no layer to crack or peel. The fabric stays as soft and breathable as it was before printing.

When sublimation is perfect, and when to choose another method

Sublimation gives stunning results on the right fabric and can disappoint on the wrong one. Here is how to tell which side your order falls on.

Sublimation is the best choice when:

  • You want all-over designs, stripes, patterns or edge-to-edge colour
  • Your garments are polyester sportswear like jerseys, shorts and track jackets
  • You need names and numbers on every jersey in a team kit
  • You want a print you cannot feel, that never cracks or peels

Another method is better when:

  • Your tees are cotton, polycot or bio-wash. Sublimation does not work on cotton, so DTF printing in Dombivli is the better choice for full colour on these fabrics.
  • You want a dark coloured base fabric like black or navy. Sublimation needs white or light fabric, though a dark design printed all over can give a dark look.
  • You want a stitched logo on a track jacket or polo. Embroidery in Dombivli adds that premium, textured finish.
  • You are ordering many cotton tees with a simple 1 to 3 colour design, where screen printing costs less.

A neat trick for dark kits is to design a deep navy or black background as part of the artwork and sublimate it onto white polyester. The whole jersey looks dark, while the white fabric underneath lets the colours stay accurate.

Artwork tips for brilliant sublimation prints

Sublimation can reproduce almost any design, which makes the artwork stage the fun part. A few habits help everything print just right.

Design for the full garment

For all-over jerseys, think of the design as covering the whole front, back and sleeves. Share a reference image or sketch of what you like, and we place it on the garment in the mockup so you can see the full look.

Remember that white is the fabric

Sublimation does not print white ink. Any white areas in your design simply show the white polyester underneath. That is perfect on a white base, so keep it in mind if you were planning a white logo on a coloured panel.

Make names and numbers easy to read

Pick a bold, clear font for names and numbers so umpires, referees and spectators can read them from the boundary. Strong contrast between the number and the background colour matters more than a fancy font.

Send a clear player list

Share a list with each player's name, number and size in one row. Check spellings and duplicate numbers before sending, since we print exactly what the list says.

Use high resolution artwork

Large designs need large files. Vector files or high resolution images keep edges sharp across a whole jersey. Low resolution logos can look blurry when stretched to fill a big panel.

Fabric pairing for sublimation

Sublimation has one golden rule: polyester only, in white or light colours. The dye bonds only with polyester fibres, so cotton simply will not hold the colour.

  • Polyester jerseys are the classic pairing, light, quick drying and ideal for long matches in Dombivli's humid weather.
  • Polyester shorts match the jersey perfectly, so the whole kit carries the same colours.
  • Polyester round neck tees suit garba groups, running clubs and sports day participants who want an all-over pattern.
  • Polyester track jackets take sublimated stripes and panels for a sporty warm-up look.

If you want a particular fabric weight for your kit, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Because the colour is inside the fibre, a sublimated jersey keeps its design through a full season of matches and washes. These steps help it look fresh even longer.

  • Wash in cold or normal water with a mild detergent.
  • Turn the kit inside out to reduce rubbing from other clothes.
  • Avoid bleach and strong stain removers on the coloured areas.
  • Dry in the shade, since polyester dries quickly anyway.
  • Avoid very hot irons. Polyester rarely needs ironing, and high heat can mark it.

Look after it this way and your team kit will still look sharp in the final, months after the first match.

How much does sublimation printing cost in Dombivli?

Sublimation starts from ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. A sports jersey starts at ₹200, so a front and back sublimated jersey starts at ₹250 per piece. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp.

Can sublimation be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation only works on polyester, because the dye bonds with polyester fibres. For full colour designs on cotton, DTF printing is the better option.

What is the minimum order for sublimated jerseys in Dombivli?

Sublimated sports jerseys start from 5 pieces. Custom shorts, polyester tees and track jackets also start from 5 pieces each.

Can I get different names and numbers on each cricket jersey?

Yes. Sublimation prints each jersey individually, so every player can have a different name and number. Send a list with names, numbers and sizes on WhatsApp.

Can sublimation print on black or dark jerseys?

Sublimation needs white or light polyester fabric. To get a dark look, we design a dark background into the artwork and sublimate it onto white fabric, so the whole jersey appears dark.

Does a sublimated jersey fade or crack after washing?

No. The design is dyed into the fibres, so there is nothing on top to crack or peel. Washing inside out in cold water and avoiding bleach keeps the colours bright for longer.
