How screen printing works, from stencil to finished tee

Screen printing is one of the oldest garment printing methods, and the idea is wonderfully simple. Ink is pushed through a fine mesh onto the fabric, and a stencil decides where it goes. Here is how your design travels from file to tee:

  1. Colour separation. Your artwork is split into its individual colours. A two colour logo becomes two separate layers.
  2. Making the screens. Each colour gets its own mesh screen. A light sensitive coating is applied, the layer is exposed onto it, and the open areas form the stencil.
  3. Lining up the garment. The tee is laid flat on a printing board, and the screen is placed precisely over the print area.
  4. Printing. Ink is poured on the screen and pulled across with a squeegee, pressing it through the open mesh onto the fabric. For more colours, each screen is printed in turn and lined up carefully.
  5. Curing. The printed garment goes through heat, which sets the ink so it bonds with the fibres and survives washing.

The screens take effort to prepare, but once they are ready, every extra tee is quick to print. That is exactly why the method gets cheaper per piece on bigger runs.

When screen printing wins, and when to choose another method

Screen printing is the value champion for the right kind of order. For the wrong kind, another method will look better or cost less.

Screen printing is ideal when:

  • You are ordering around 50 pieces or more
  • Your design uses 1 to 3 flat, solid colours
  • Every piece carries the same design
  • You are printing on cotton, polycot or other blends
  • You want a thick, opaque print that stands out from a distance

Another method is better when:

  • Your design has photos, gradients or lots of colours, or each piece needs a different name. DTF printing in South Mumbai handles all of that with no setup.
  • You want all-over designs on polyester sportswear, such as football or cricket jerseys. Sublimation printing in South Mumbai dyes the design into the fabric so it never cracks.
  • You need a stitched logo on polos, shirts or caps for a formal office look. Embroidery suits that brief best.

A handy rule of thumb is to count your colours and your pieces. Few colours and many pieces point to screen printing. Many colours and few pieces point to DTF.

Artwork tips for clean screen prints

Screen printing rewards clean, confident artwork. A little preparation means sharper edges and an easier approval.

  • Send a vector file if you can. AI, EPS, PDF or SVG files keep edges crisp at any size. A high resolution PNG also works, and we will check it on the mockup.
  • Count your colours. Each colour needs its own screen, so keep the design to 1 to 3 solid colours for the best value.
  • Avoid gradients and soft shadows. Screen printing lays down solid ink, so fades and photo effects do not translate well. If your design needs them, DTF is the answer.
  • Mention exact colours. If your mandal saffron or company blue matters, share the Pantone code or a reference image.
  • Think about the garment colour. White ink on a dark tee and dark ink on a light tee give the strongest contrast. Very similar shades can disappear into the fabric.
  • Keep lettering bold. Thick letters read well from across a street or a sports ground. Thin script fonts can look faint at a distance.

Fabric pairing for screen printing

Screen printing ink grips natural fibres especially well, which is why cotton and blends give the cleanest results.

  • Cotton absorbs the ink beautifully and stays comfortable through a long procession or a hot sports day.
  • Polycot blends cotton with polyester, so the tee holds its shape and wrinkles less, while still taking ink nicely.
  • Bio-wash cotton has a smooth surface that gives crisp edges on detailed logos.
  • Sinker is a soft knit that works well for hoodies printed for cold air conditioned offices.
  • Canvas on tote bags takes a bold, one colour print wonderfully.

Fabric weight also changes how a tee feels and wears. A heavier tee gives a solid, structured base for a big back print, while a lighter one stays cooler on a humid festival day. Tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for screen printed tees

Screen printed designs are known for lasting a long time, because the cured ink bonds into the fibres. Plenty of people still wear festival and college tees printed years ago.

Caring for your prints

  • Wash inside out in cold or lukewarm water
  • Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach
  • Dry in the shade, especially for dark colours
  • Iron inside out, never directly on the print
  • Avoid tumble drying on high heat

What changes with time

A thick print may show tiny fine lines after many washes, mostly where the fabric stretches the most. That is natural for the method and does not affect how the design reads. Following the care steps above keeps it looking bold for much longer.
