How DTF printing works, step by step

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, the design is printed onto a thin film first and then transferred onto the garment with heat. Here is what happens at our unit once you approve your mockup:

  1. Printing on film. Your design is printed in full colour onto a special clear film, with a layer of white ink laid behind the colours.
  2. Adding the adhesive. While the ink is still wet, a fine adhesive powder is spread over it and the extra is shaken off.
  3. Curing. The powder is melted with gentle heat, so the film now carries a ready transfer.
  4. Pressing. The transfer is placed on the garment and pressed with a heat press, which bonds the adhesive into the fibres.
  5. Peeling. The film is peeled away, leaving your design on the fabric, and a final press sets it.

That white layer behind the colours is the secret to why DTF looks so bright on black, navy and maroon fabric. And because there are no screens or frames to prepare, one tee and one hundred tees go through exactly the same process. When DTF is the right pick, and when another method wins

DTF is wonderfully flexible, but it is not the answer to every order. Here is how we usually guide people in the area.

Choose DTF when:

  • Your design has many colours, a photo, a gradient or fine colourful detail.
  • You need fewer than about 50 pieces.
  • Every piece needs a different name or number.
  • You are printing on dark fabric and want the colours to stay bright.
  • Your order mixes fabrics, such as cotton tees and polyester shorts with the same logo.

Look at another method when:

  • You are ordering 50 or more tees with a simple 1 to 3 colour design. Screen printing will usually cost less per piece, and our page on screen printing in Mira Road explains why.
  • You want an all-over design on a polyester sports jersey. Sublimation dyes the design right into the fabric, so take a look at sublimation printing in Mira Road.
  • You want a raised, stitched logo for a premium look on polos or caps. That is a job for embroidery.

If you are still unsure, our comparison of DTF vs screen printing walks through the numbers. Or simply send us your design and quantity, and we will tell you which one makes more sense.

Artwork tips that make DTF prints look sharp

DTF reproduces your file very faithfully, which is lovely when the file is good and less lovely when it is blurry. A few small habits make a big difference.

  • Send a high resolution file. A PNG with a transparent background works best, ideally 300 dpi at the size it will be printed. Vector files such as PDF or AI are perfect for logos.
  • Remove the background. A white box around your logo will print as a white box on a coloured tee. If you are not sure how to remove it, tell us and we will help.
  • Send photos as documents on WhatsApp. Forwarding a photo normally compresses it. Choosing the document option keeps the original quality, which matters a lot for family photos.
  • Avoid very thin lines and tiny text. Hairline strokes and isolated specks are delicate on any transfer, so keep lines reasonably bold.
  • Share names in a simple list. For name orders, send a sheet with name, number if any, and size for each person, and double check spellings before you send it.
  • Tell us the print size. A small chest logo and a full front design are both one position, but knowing the size helps us set up your mockup correctly.

Making your DTF print last: wash and care

A well pressed DTF print stays bright and flexible through regular wear, and a little care helps it look new for longer.

  • Wait a day after receiving the garment before the first wash.
  • Turn it inside out and wash in cold or lukewarm water with a mild detergent.
  • Skip bleach and strong stain removers on the printed area.
  • Dry in the shade. During the monsoon, drying indoors under a fan is perfectly fine.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron the garment inside out, or place a cloth over the design.
  • Avoid wringing or stretching the print hard while it is wet.

Treat it like your favourite tee, and the gang cartoon will still be grinning at your reunion years from now.
