How DTF printing works, step by step

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, we print your design onto a special film first and then transfer it to the garment with heat and pressure. It sounds technical, but the steps are simple.

  1. Your artwork is printed onto film. The printer lays down your design in full colour, with a white layer behind it so the colours stay bright on any fabric shade.
  2. Adhesive powder is added. A fine powder is spread over the wet ink and melted, which turns the print into a flexible transfer that can grip fabric.
  3. The transfer is pressed onto the garment. We place it on the exact print position you approved in your mockup and press it with heat.
  4. The film is peeled away. What stays behind is your design, bonded to the fabric, with every gradient and fine detail in place.

Because there are no screens or stencils to prepare, each piece can carry a different design. That is why DTF is such a favourite for names, photos and small batches where every tee tells its own story.

When to choose DTF, and when another method is better

DTF is wonderful, but it is not always the cheapest or the most suitable option. Here is an honest way to decide.

Choose DTF when:

  • Your design has a photo, gradients, shading or more than 3 colours
  • You need a different name or number on each piece
  • Your order is small, from 2 custom printed tees or 5 of most other products
  • You are printing on a mix of fabrics, or on dark colours with bright artwork

Pick another method when:

  • You are ordering about 50 or more pieces of a simple 1 to 3 colour design. Screen printing in Dombivli brings the cost per piece down a lot on bigger runs.
  • You want a stitched, premium logo on polos, shirts or caps. Embroidery in Dombivli gives that textured, office-ready finish.
  • You want an all-over design on a white or light polyester sports jersey. Sublimation dyes the design into the fabric and suits full kits.

If you are still unsure, send us the design on WhatsApp. We will look at the colours, the quantity and the fabric and suggest what gives you the best result for the money.

Artwork and file tips for sharp DTF prints

DTF reproduces whatever you send very faithfully, which is great news for good artwork and a little risky for blurry files. A few simple habits make a big difference.

Send the best version of your photo

Photos shared on WhatsApp as images get compressed, and the print can look soft. Send the photo as a document instead, so the original file reaches us. If you only have an old printed photo, scan it rather than photographing it under a tube light.

Use a transparent background for logos

A PNG with a transparent background lets the garment colour show around your logo. If your file has a white box behind it, we can usually remove it, but a clean file from the start always looks crisper.

Type out every name and check spellings

For name printed orders, share a simple list with each name next to its size. Double check spellings of names like "Aishwarya" or "Siddhesh" before sending, since we print exactly what the list says. Our guide to name printed t-shirts has more tips on fonts and placement.

Keep tiny details readable

Very thin lines and tiny text can print, but they get hard to read from a distance. On a back print, keep names large enough to read from across a room. Your mockup will show you the size before anything is made. For photo-heavy designs, our page on photo printing on t-shirts explains how to pick the right image.

Fabric pairing for DTF prints

One of the nicest things about DTF is how flexible it is with fabric. Because the print bonds on top of the fibres rather than relying on the fabric's makeup, it works on cotton, polyester, sinker, polycot and bio-wash, in light or dark colours.

  • Cotton and bio-wash are the most popular for family tees and farewell tees, soft and comfortable through Dombivli's humid months.
  • Polyester suits sports tees and hi-vis jackets, and DTF sits well on it without needing a white base fabric.
  • Polycot keeps its shape well wash after wash, which makes it a good choice for staff tees with department names.
  • Sinker has a soft, casual feel that teenagers in particular seem to love.

For the softest feel, keep very large solid blocks of colour to a sensible size. A huge solid rectangle feels heavier than a design with some open space in it. Fabric weight matters too, so tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for DTF prints

A well pressed DTF print stretches with the fabric and holds its colours through many washes. A little care keeps it looking new for much longer.

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Use a normal or cold wash and a mild detergent, and skip bleach.
  • Dry in the shade rather than in harsh afternoon sun.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron inside out or place a cloth over the design.
  • Avoid tumble drying on high heat.

Follow these and your farewell tee or family photo tee can stay in the cupboard rotation for years, which is the whole point of a keepsake.

Can DTF print a family photo on a t-shirt?

Yes, photos are one of the things DTF handles best, with full colour, skin tones and soft shading. Send the original photo as a document on WhatsApp rather than as a compressed image so it prints sharply.

Can each t-shirt have a different name with DTF printing?

Does DTF printing work on dark coloured t-shirts?

Yes. DTF prints a white layer behind your colours, so bright designs stay vivid on black, navy, maroon and other dark shades. It works on cotton, polyester, polycot, sinker and bio-wash.
