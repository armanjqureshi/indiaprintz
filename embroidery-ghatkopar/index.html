How embroidery works, from logo to stitched garment

Embroidery uses a computer guided machine and coloured thread to sew your logo directly into the fabric. Getting there takes a little more preparation than printing, which is part of why it looks so refined.

  1. Your logo is digitised. We convert the artwork into a stitch file. This file tells the machine the type of stitch, the direction of every row and how dense each area should be.
  2. Thread colours are matched. We pick the threads closest to your brand colours from our range and show them on the mockup.
  3. The garment is hooped. The polo, shirt or cap is clamped tight in a frame with a stabiliser backing underneath, so the fabric cannot shift while stitching.
  4. The machine stitches colour by colour. Each colour is sewn in turn, building the logo layer by layer.
  5. It is finished by hand. Loose threads are trimmed, extra backing is removed and the area is pressed flat for a neat look.

The digitising step is where good embroidery is won or lost. A well made stitch file keeps small letters readable and curves smooth. You can read more about the method on our main embroidery in Mumbai page.

When embroidery is right, and when to choose printing

Embroidery is a premium finish, and it is worth knowing when it really earns its place. Choose logo embroidery when:

  • The garment will be worn often, for months or years, like a uniform.
  • Your logo is simple, with clear shapes and a few solid colours.
  • The setting is client facing, such as a reception, clinic or counter.
  • The garment is a polo, shirt, cap or jacket with some weight to it.
  • You want a raised, textured look that a print cannot copy.

Printing is the better call in some cases. Photos, gradients and very fine text cannot be embroidered, so detailed artwork belongs with DTF printing in Ghatkopar, which reproduces every shade. For a one day event where 300 people need the same simple tee, screen printing in Ghatkopar keeps the cost per piece much lower. A popular mix is an embroidered chest logo on the front with a printed design on the back.

Getting your logo ready for embroidery

Thread behaves very differently from ink, so a logo that prints well does not always stitch well. These tips help your logo come out sharp.

  • Send a vector logo if you have one. AI, PDF, EPS or SVG files give us clean shapes to digitise. A clear, high resolution PNG also works.
  • Keep it simple. Solid shapes and clean lines stitch beautifully. Thin outlines, tiny details and shading tend to blur together.
  • Watch the text size. Very small letters fill in and become unreadable. If a tagline is tiny under your logo, consider leaving it off the stitched version.
  • Limit the colours. Most logos look their best in 2 to 5 thread colours. Gradients cannot be stitched, so they are converted into solid bands of colour.
  • Think about the size. A left chest logo is usually about the width of a credit card. Caps and sleeves need even smaller, simpler versions.
  • Tell us your brand colours. Share the Pantone or hex codes, and we match them to the closest threads on the mockup.

Fabrics that suit embroidery

Embroidery needs a fabric strong enough to hold thousands of stitches. Polos in cotton, matty, polycot or bio-wash are ideal, because the knit is firm and the logo sits flat. Corporate shirts in cotton or polycot take a chest logo neatly and look sharp under office lights.

Caps, hoodies and jackets are perfect partners as well, since their heavier fabrics carry dense embroidery without puckering. Lightweight round neck tees can be embroidered, but a large dense logo on thin fabric may pull slightly, so we usually suggest a smaller design or a printed method for those.

If your team has a preferred fabric weight for polos, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability. The right weight makes embroidery look even richer.

How long embroidery lasts, and how to care for it

Embroidery is the longest lasting decoration we offer. The thread is sewn into the fabric, so there is nothing to crack, peel or fade off the surface. Most embroidered logos outlive the garment they are on.

  • Wash inside out, in cold or lukewarm water.
  • Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach, which can dull thread colours.
  • Do not scrub the embroidered area hard.
  • Dry flat or on a hanger in the shade.
  • Iron on the reverse side, ideally with a cloth between the iron and the logo.
  • If a stray thread appears, trim it with scissors rather than pulling it.

Staff uniforms that go through the wash several times a week stay presentable with these habits, which is why clinics and offices keep coming back to embroidery.
