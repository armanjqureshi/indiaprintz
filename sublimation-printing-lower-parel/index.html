How sublimation printing in Lower Parel works, step by step

Sublimation uses heat to turn special inks into a gas that bonds with polyester. Here is how your design becomes a finished jersey at our unit.

  1. Design set up. Your artwork is laid out to fit the garment panels or print area, including any all-over pattern, names and numbers.
  2. Printing on transfer paper. The design is printed in mirror image onto special paper using sublimation inks.
  3. Heat pressing. The paper is placed on white or light polyester fabric and pressed at high heat.
  4. The ink changes state. Under that heat, the ink turns into a gas and soaks into the opened polyester fibres.
  5. Cooling and sealing. As the fabric cools, the fibres close around the colour, locking it in. The paper is peeled away and the design is now part of the fabric.

Because the colour lives inside the fibres, a sublimated garment has no print layer at all. Run your hand across it and you feel only the fabric.

Why sublimation needs white or light polyester

Sublimation inks are transparent, a little like watercolours. They show up beautifully on white and pale fabric, but they cannot cover a dark base. That is why a sublimated navy jersey is actually white polyester printed navy all over. The inks also bond only with polyester fibres. On cotton, the colour simply washes out, so sublimation does not work on cotton garments.

Sublimation or another method: making the call

Sublimation is unbeatable for some jobs and simply wrong for others. Here is a quick way to decide.

Sublimation is the right choice when:

  • The garment is polyester in white or a light colour
  • You want all-over designs, gradients or full colour patterns
  • You need a print that never cracks, peels or feels heavy
  • The kit will see a lot of sweat and frequent washing

If your team wants cotton tees, or a dark coloured garment with a full colour logo, DTF printing in Lower Parel is the better choice, since it works on most fabrics and colours. For coaches' polos or caps with a premium stitched logo, embroidery in Lower Parel adds a polished finish that pairs nicely with a sublimated playing kit.

Artwork tips for sublimation

Sublimation can print almost anything, so your artwork can be as bold as you like. A few tips help it come out perfectly.

  • Go big with colour. Gradients, photos, textures and all-over patterns all print well, so this is the method to be ambitious with.
  • Send high resolution files. For all-over designs, artwork should be large and sharp at the actual print size. Vector files or 300 dpi images are ideal.
  • Keep important details away from seams. On all-over designs, logos and numbers should sit clear of side seams and underarms where the fabric folds.
  • Avoid white as a design colour on coloured backgrounds. Sublimation has no white ink. Any white in your design is simply the fabric showing through, so plan white areas with that in mind.
  • Share a player list. For jerseys, send names, numbers and sizes in one spreadsheet so each piece is matched correctly.
  • Give us your brand colours. Pantone or hex codes help us get the shade right.

Fabric pairing: polyester, light colours and comfort

Sublimation works on polyester only, and on white or light fabric. That might sound limiting, but polyester is exactly what sportswear needs. It is light, it wicks sweat away from the skin and it dries quickly in Mumbai humidity, which matters for evening matches and early runs alike.

For jerseys and running tees, a smooth polyester gives the sharpest print. Polyester polos suit corporate tournaments where teams want a collar. Track jackets in polyester carry sublimated crests and names cleanly. If your group insists on cotton for comfort, choose DTF instead, since sublimation will not hold on cotton. How long sublimation lasts, and wash care

Sublimation is the most fade resistant way to add colour to polyester. Because the dye is inside the fibres, there is nothing on the surface to crack, peel or rub off, even after a season of matches.

A few simple habits keep kits looking new:

  • Wash soon after matches so sweat does not sit in the fabric
  • Use cold water and a mild detergent, inside out
  • Skip bleach and fabric softener, which can dull polyester
  • Dry in the shade, away from harsh direct sun
  • Keep the iron away from the print, or use a low setting on the reverse

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Lower Parel?

Sublimation starts from ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. For 15 sports jerseys with a front and back print, the starting price is (200 + 25 x 2) x 15 = ₹3,750. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp.

Can sublimation be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation inks bond only with polyester fibres, so the colour does not hold on cotton. For cotton tees, DTF or screen printing is the right choice.

Can you sublimate a black or navy jersey?

Sublimation needs white or light polyester as the base. A dark jersey is made by printing the dark colour all over white polyester as part of the design, so you still get a navy or black kit.

What is the minimum order for sublimated jerseys in Worli?

Sports jerseys start at 5 pieces. Matching shorts and track jackets also start at 5 pieces, so a small five-a-side squad can order a full kit.

Can you print names and numbers on sublimated jerseys?

Yes. Each jersey can carry a different name and number. Send a spreadsheet with names, numbers and sizes so we can match every piece correctly.

Will a sublimated jersey crack or fade?

Sublimated prints do not crack or peel because the colour is inside the fibres. Wash in cold water and dry in the shade to keep the colours bright for a long time.

Is sublimation good for running club t-shirts?

Yes. Polyester running tees dry quickly and sublimated designs feel weightless, which runners appreciate on long, humid mornings along the Sea Face.
