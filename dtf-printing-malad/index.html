How DTF printing works, in plain words

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, the design goes onto a clear film first and is then pressed onto the garment. Here is the journey your artwork takes.

  1. The design is printed onto film. A special printer lays down your full colour artwork on a PET film, followed by a layer of white ink behind it so the colours stay bright on dark fabric.
  2. Adhesive powder is added. While the ink is still wet, a fine powder is spread over it. It sticks only to the printed areas.
  3. The film is cured. Heat melts the powder into a thin, even glue layer on the back of the print.
  4. It is heat pressed onto the garment. The film is placed on the tee, hoodie or bag and pressed with heat and pressure, so the print bonds with the fabric.
  5. The film is peeled away. What remains is your design, sitting cleanly on the garment with every colour in place.

Because nothing has to be set up per colour, a photo with hundreds of shades costs the same to print as a single colour logo of the same size. That is the secret behind its popularity, and our main guide to DTF printing in Mumbai goes deeper into the process.

When DTF is the right call, and when another method wins

Every method has a sweet spot, and we would always rather recommend the right one than the one you asked for. Here is how we think it through.

Choose DTF when

  • Your design has many colours, gradients, shadows or a photo
  • Every piece needs a different name, number or role
  • You need a small quantity, from 2 to around 50 pieces
  • The garment is a blend or polyester where other methods struggle
  • You want one order to mix tees, hoodies and totes with the same artwork

Choose something else when

For 50 or more tees with a bold logo in 1 to 3 flat colours, screen printing usually works out cheaper per piece. For polos, shirts and caps where you want a raised, premium finish, embroidery in Malad is the classier choice. And for sports jerseys with all-over patterns on white polyester, sublimation is made for the job.

Artwork and file tips for sharp DTF prints

DTF is forgiving, but a good file still makes a visible difference. A few habits help your design come out exactly as you pictured it.

  • Send the highest resolution you have. A PNG at 300 dpi at the actual print size is ideal. A vector file such as AI, PDF or SVG is even better for logos and text.
  • Use a transparent background. If the design has a white box around it, that box will be printed too unless you tell us to remove it.
  • Watch very thin lines. Lines thinner than about 1 mm can look fragile. Thicken them slightly if you can.
  • Check photo quality early. Photos taken on a phone in good light print well. Screenshots of photos from chats are often too small and look soft.
  • Spell names exactly. For named orders, send the list in a simple spreadsheet or typed message, and double check spellings before you approve the mockup.

If your file is not perfect, send it anyway. We will tell you honestly whether it will print well and suggest what to fix.

Fabrics that pair well with DTF

One of the best things about DTF is how few fabric limits it has. It bonds to cotton, polyester, polycot, sinker and bio-wash, on light and dark colours alike.

Cotton and bio-wash give the softest feel and suit everyday tees and family trip merch. Polycot holds its shape and resists wrinkles, which is handy for office tees that get worn every week. Polyester dries fast, so it suits promoters and outdoor events near the sea at Marve and Madh. For hoodies, sinker and cotton feel cosy in cold Mindspace air conditioning.

The print sits on the surface of the fabric with a smooth, slightly soft feel. On very large designs that cover most of the back, you will feel it more than you would a screen print in thin ink, so we often suggest a sensible print size. Making DTF prints last: wash care

A DTF print looks its best for longest when it is washed with a little care. None of this is hard, and it quickly becomes habit.

  • Wash the garment inside out in cold or lukewarm water.
  • Use a mild detergent and skip bleach.
  • Avoid wringing the printed area hard.
  • Dry in the shade rather than harsh afternoon sun.
  • Iron inside out, or with a cloth over the print, never directly on it.
  • Wait a day after receiving the tee before the first wash.

Follow these and your team tees, batch hoodies and family trip merch will stay bright through many wears.
