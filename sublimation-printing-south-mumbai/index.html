How sublimation printing works, explained simply

Sublimation uses heat to turn special ink into a gas that bonds with polyester fibres. It sounds technical, but the steps are easy to follow.

  1. The design is laid out on a garment template. Your artwork is placed on a flat pattern of the jersey, shorts or jacket, so every panel, stripe and number lands in the right spot.
  2. It is printed onto transfer paper. Sublimation inks are printed onto special paper, in mirror image, so it reads correctly once transferred.
  3. The paper meets the fabric. The printed paper is placed against white or light polyester fabric or a finished garment.
  4. Heat and pressure do the work. A heat press warms the ink until it turns into a gas, which travels into the polyester fibres and settles there as the fabric cools.
  5. The garment is finished. For full kits, the printed panels are cut and stitched into jerseys or shorts at our unit, then checked and packed.

The result is a print you cannot feel with your fingers. The fabric stays light and breathable, which matters a lot when you are playing in Mumbai humidity.

When sublimation is the best choice, and when it is not

Sublimation is unbeatable for some jobs and simply wrong for others. A quick check of fabric and colour usually decides it.

Sublimation is perfect when:

  • The garment is polyester, such as jerseys, sports tees and track jackets
  • The base colour is white or light
  • You want all-over designs, stripes, gradients or photos across the whole garment
  • The kit will face sweat, sun and frequent washing
  • You need names and numbers that never crack or peel

Choose a different method when:

  • You want cotton tees. Sublimation does not work on cotton, and DTF printing in South Mumbai prints full colour designs on cotton beautifully.
  • You want a dark garment, such as a black jersey with a small logo. Sublimation ink cannot print light colours onto dark fabric, so DTF is the better pick there too.
  • You want a premium stitched crest on a club polo or a cap. Embroidery in South Mumbai gives that raised, classic finish.

Many teams combine methods, with sublimated match jerseys and embroidered caps or polos for the club committee.

Artwork and file tips for sublimation

Sublimated garments often carry designs that cover every panel, so the artwork needs a bit more planning than a simple chest logo.

  • Design on a template. Ask us for the garment template, or share your idea and we will place it on one for the mockup. This keeps stripes and logos clear of seams and underarms.
  • Extend backgrounds past the edges. If your design covers the whole panel, let the colour run a little beyond the cut line so there are no white gaps at the seams.
  • Remember there is no white ink. Any part of your design that is white will simply show the fabric colour. On white polyester that looks perfect, while on a light grey base the white areas will read as grey.
  • Use high resolution files. Vector files or large PNG and PDF files keep crests, sponsor logos and numbers crisp across the full garment.
  • Send a player list. For team kits, share each player's name, number and size in a simple list, with spellings exactly as they should appear.
  • Expect bright colours. Sublimation gives rich, vivid colour. If an exact club shade matters, share a colour code or reference image.

Fabric pairing for sublimation

This is the one method where fabric decides everything. Sublimation ink bonds only with polyester fibres, so the right base is essential.

  • Polyester is the ideal base, giving the brightest colours and the sharpest detail. It is also light and quick drying, which suits football, cricket and running.
  • Matty is a textured polyester that looks great on polos and jerseys and takes sublimation well.
  • White and light colours give the best results. The lighter the base, the truer your colours.
  • Cotton is not suitable. The ink washes out of cotton fibres, so for cotton garments we recommend DTF or screen printing instead.

Fit matters as much as fabric for sportswear. Our size chart and fabric guide lists chest, length and sleeve measurements so every player picks the right size. Tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Sublimation is one of the toughest prints you can choose for sportswear. The ink is inside the fibres, so there is no layer on top that can crack, flake or peel, even after a season of matches and washes.

Care that keeps colours bright

  • Wash inside out in cold water after every match or run
  • Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach
  • Dry in the shade, since strong sun over many months can soften bright colours
  • Iron only on low heat if needed, as polyester does not like high temperatures
  • Keep hot items, such as a pressing iron or a very hot dryer, away from the printed areas

What lasts and what to watch

Names, numbers and stripes stay sharp and flexible for the life of the garment. The main thing to protect is the polyester itself, so gentle washing and low heat keep the whole kit looking match ready.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in South Mumbai?

Sublimation starts at ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. For example, 40 polyester running tees printed front and back start at ₹6,800. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp once we see your design.

Can sublimation printing be done on cotton t-shirts?

No, sublimation only works on polyester. The ink bonds with polyester fibres and does not hold on cotton. For cotton tees, DTF or screen printing is the right choice.

Can I get sublimated football jerseys for a team at the Cooperage?

Yes, football kits are one of the most popular sublimation orders. Jerseys and shorts start at 5 pieces each, with names and numbers for every player. Send your design and player list for a quote and dispatch date.

Does sublimation work on black jerseys?

Sublimation works best on white or light polyester, because the ink cannot print light colours onto dark fabric. You can still have a dark look by printing a dark design across a white base. For a black garment with a small logo, DTF is a better fit.

Do sublimated prints crack or fade?

Sublimated prints do not crack or peel, because the ink is inside the fibres rather than on top. Washing in cold water and drying in the shade keeps colours bright for a long time.
