How screen printing works, step by step

The idea behind screen printing is very old and very simple: push ink through a fine mesh stencil onto fabric. Doing it well takes practice, and here is what happens to your design.

  1. Separating the colours. Your artwork is split into its individual colours. A two colour logo becomes two separate layers.
  2. Making the screens. Each colour gets its own mesh screen, coated with a light sensitive emulsion. The design is exposed onto it and washed out, leaving open mesh only where the ink should go.
  3. Lining up. The screens are mounted on a press and carefully aligned so every colour lands exactly in place.
  4. Printing. The garment is laid flat, and ink is pulled across the screen with a squeegee, pressing it through the open mesh. This repeats for each colour.
  5. Curing. The printed garment passes through heat so the ink sets properly into the fabric.

Steps 1 to 3 are the setup. They take the same effort for 10 tees as for 300, which is exactly why screen printing becomes so economical on bigger runs. Once the screens are on the press, each extra tee is quick to print.

When to pick screen printing, and when to pick something else

Every method has its sweet spot. Here is a quick, honest way to decide.

Screen printing is the right pick when:

  • You need around 50 pieces or more of the same design
  • The design uses 1 to 3 flat colours
  • You are printing on cotton, polycot, bio-wash or sinker
  • You want a print that feels solid and looks strong from a distance
  • You may reorder the same design later in the year

Another method is the better pick when:

  • Your design has photos, gradients or lots of colours, or you need fewer than about 50 pieces. DTF printing in Kurla handles full colour with no setup.
  • Each piece needs a different name or number. DTF is easier here too.
  • You want a stitched logo on office polos, caps or jackets. Embroidery in Kurla gives a premium, textured finish.
  • You are making all-over polyester sports jerseys, where sublimation is the natural fit.

Artwork tips for crisp screen prints

Screen printing rewards clean, confident artwork. A few minutes spent on your file can make the finished tee look far better.

  • Keep the colour count low. Each colour needs its own screen, so 1 to 3 flat colours is the sweet spot. If your logo has six shades, we can help simplify it for print.
  • Avoid gradients and photos. Smooth shading and photographs do not suit flat colour screens. If your design depends on them, DTF is the better route.
  • Send a vector file. PDF, AI, EPS or SVG files give razor sharp edges. A high resolution PNG works too, and we will let you know if it needs cleaning up.
  • Name your colours. If your brand has exact colours, share the codes. Ink colours can be matched closely, and the mockup shows what to expect.
  • Give thin lines some weight. Very fine lines and tiny text can fill in or break up on fabric. Bolder strokes print more reliably.
  • Think about the garment colour. A light design on a dark tee needs a white base layer under the colour, so mention your tee colour early and we plan for it.

Fabric pairing for screen printing

Screen printing ink grips natural fibres beautifully, which is why cotton and blends are its best partners. Plain cotton round necks give the classic soft, matte result. Bio-wash cotton has a smoother surface that shows fine edges nicely. Polycot holds its shape through many washes, ideal for staff tees and polos. Sinker hoodies take a screen print well on the front or back.

Pure polyester is a different story. Its fibres do not hold screen ink as reliably as cotton, so for polyester tees, jackets and sportswear we usually suggest DTF, or sublimation for light coloured kits.

Fabric weight affects comfort in Kurla's humid months as much as the fibre does. Tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

How long screen prints last, and how to wash them

Screen printing has a well earned reputation for durability. The ink is laid down in a solid layer and set with heat, so a good print stays bright through a lot of wearing and washing. Many people find their old screen printed event tees still going strong years later.

To keep yours looking that way:

  • Turn the tee inside out before every wash.
  • Use cold or normal temperature water.
  • Choose a mild detergent and avoid bleach.
  • Line dry in the shade instead of under a harsh afternoon sun.
  • Iron on the reverse side, never directly on the print.

For mandals and societies, it helps to send these points in your volunteer group when you hand out the tees, so they last for next year's photos too.
