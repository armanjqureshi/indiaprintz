What DTF printing is and how it works

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, the design is first printed onto a thin film and then transferred onto the garment with heat. It sounds technical, but the steps are easy to follow.

  1. Printing the film. Your artwork is printed in full colour onto a special film, with a layer of white ink behind it so the colours stay bright on any shade of fabric.
  2. Adding the adhesive. While the ink is still wet, a fine adhesive powder is spread across the back of the print. It sticks only where there is ink.
  3. Curing the transfer. The film is heated so the powder melts into a smooth layer. At this point the design is a ready transfer, waiting for its garment.
  4. Pressing it on. The transfer is placed on the tee, hoodie or bag and pressed with heat and pressure, which bonds the adhesive into the fabric.
  5. Peeling the film. Once it cools, the film is peeled away and only your design stays behind on the garment.

The big advantage is that there are no screens to make. Every transfer can be different, so a name, a number or a whole new image on each piece costs nothing extra in setup. Photos, gradients, fine detail and dozens of colours all print in a single pass, which is why DTF is the go-to for artwork that would be impossible or very costly to screen print. When DTF is the right pick, and when it is not

DTF is wonderfully flexible, but it is not always the cheapest or the most suitable choice. A good rule is to let the artwork and the quantity decide.

DTF is usually the best choice when:

  • your design has photos, gradients, shadows or many colours
  • each piece needs a different name, number or role
  • the order is small, from a couple of pieces to a few dozen
  • you want to print on dark and light garments in the same order
  • the fabric is a blend or polyester that other methods struggle with

For a big run of simple artwork, such as 150 tees with a one or two colour logo, screen printing usually works out cheaper per piece. You can read more about that on our page for screen printing in Andheri. And if the goal is a premium logo on staff polos or caps for a front desk, stitched thread will look and feel more upmarket, so take a look at embroidery in Andheri before you decide.

Artwork tips for sharp DTF prints

DTF reproduces what you give it with a lot of accuracy, so a good file makes a visible difference. A few habits help every time.

  • Send the highest quality file you have. A vector file (AI, PDF or SVG) or a PNG at a high resolution, ideally sized close to the print size, gives the cleanest edges.
  • Use a transparent background. If your PNG has a white box behind the design, that box will print. Removing it before you send the file saves a round of changes.
  • Watch very thin lines and tiny text. Hairline strokes and lettering smaller than a few millimetres can look faint once pressed. Thickening them slightly makes a big difference.
  • Keep soft fades in mind. Glows and shadows that fade to nothing can show a faint edge. Tell us if your design has them and we will suggest a small adjustment.
  • Share names in a spreadsheet. For personalised orders, a simple sheet with name, role or number and size for each person avoids spelling mistakes.

Photos print very well with DTF as long as they are sharp and well lit. If you are planning a tee around a picture, our guide to photo printing on t-shirts has more tips, and our page on name printed t-shirts covers fonts and placements for personalised orders.

Fabrics that pair well with DTF

One of the best things about DTF is how forgiving it is with fabric. Cotton, polyester, sinker, polycot and bio-wash all take a DTF transfer well, and so do canvas tote bags. Because the print has its own white base, it looks equally bright on a black hoodie, a navy tee or a white polo.

The print sits as a thin, smooth layer on top of the fabric. On small and medium designs you barely notice it, but a very large solid block of colour across the whole chest will feel a little heavier and warmer. If comfort matters, a design with some breathing space between the elements wears more softly.

For hoodies, sinker and cotton blends are lovely with DTF because the surface is smooth and the print bonds evenly. For crew tees worn through long, hot shoot days, a lighter cotton or polycot is more comfortable. Fabric weight changes how a tee drapes, so tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for DTF prints

A well pressed DTF print stretches with the fabric and holds its colour through regular washing. A little care keeps it looking new for much longer.

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Use a gentle cycle with cool or lukewarm water and a mild detergent.
  • Skip bleach and strong stain removers on the printed area.
  • Avoid a hot dryer. Drying in the shade is kinder to both the fabric and the print.
  • Never iron directly on the design. If you need to iron, do it on the reverse side or place a cloth over the print.

Treat it this way and the bright colours and fine details you approved in the mockup will stay with you through many wears and washes.

How much does DTF printing cost in Andheri?

Can I get just 2 DTF printed t-shirts in Andheri?

Yes. Custom printed t-shirts in full colour with one design start from just 2 pieces, at ₹200 per piece before printing. Most other products start from 5 pieces.

Can every t-shirt have a different name with DTF printing?

Yes, and it costs the same as printing an identical design on every piece. DTF needs no screens, so each transfer can carry its own name, role or number. Just send us a list with names and sizes.

Does DTF printing work on black t-shirts and hoodies?

It works very well. Every DTF print has a white base layer behind the colours, so the design stays bright on black, navy and other dark garments.

How long does a DTF print last?

With sensible care, a DTF print keeps its colour and detail through many washes. Wash it inside out in cool water, skip bleach and never iron directly on the design.

Is DTF or screen printing better for 20 t-shirts?

For 20 tees, DTF is usually the better choice, especially if the design has many colours or names. Screen printing becomes cheaper per piece on runs of about 50 and above with simple 1 to 3 colour artwork.
