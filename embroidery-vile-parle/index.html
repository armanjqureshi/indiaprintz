How logo embroidery works, step by step

Embroidery does not use ink at all. Your logo is built out of thousands of stitches of coloured thread, sewn directly into the fabric. Here is how it comes together.

  1. Digitising the logo. Your logo is converted into a stitch file that maps where every stitch goes, which direction it runs and which thread colour it uses. This step matters most, because a well digitised logo looks neat and sits flat.
  2. Hooping the garment. The polo, shirt or cap is held tight in a frame with a stabilising backing behind the fabric, so nothing stretches or puckers during stitching.
  3. Stitching colour by colour. The logo is sewn one thread colour at a time. Filled areas, outlines and lettering each get a stitch style that suits them.
  4. Trimming and finishing. Loose threads are trimmed, the extra backing is removed, and every piece is checked before packing.

Because everything is stitched, the result is raised, textured and very hard wearing. Our page on embroidery in Mumbai explains how we handle larger corporate uniform orders across the city.

Embroidery or a print: choosing the right finish

Embroidery is premium and durable, but it has clear limits. This is how we help people decide.

Choose embroidery when:

  • You want a premium, textured logo on polos, shirts, caps or jackets
  • The garment will be worn and washed almost daily as a uniform
  • Your logo has clear shapes and a handful of solid colours

Choose a print when:

  • Your design has photos, gradients or very fine text. DTF printing in Vile Parle handles all of these in full colour.
  • You want a large design covering the whole back of a tee. Embroidery at that size gets heavy and costly, and a print suits it better.
  • You are ordering 100 or more event tees with a bold, flat design. Screen printing is far more economical for that kind of run.
  • You want an all-over design on a polyester sports jersey, which is a job for sublimation.

Many uniform orders combine finishes, for example an embroidered logo on the chest for a premium touch and a printed role or slogan on the back. We will happily suggest a mix that suits your budget.

Artwork tips for clean logo embroidery

A logo that looks perfect on a business card does not always stitch perfectly, because thread has thickness and needs space. These tips help.

  • Send the cleanest file you have. A vector file in AI, EPS, PDF or SVG is best. A high resolution PNG also works.
  • Simplify fine details. Very fine text and photos cannot be embroidered. A tiny tagline under the logo often needs to be enlarged or left out, and we will tell you honestly on the mockup.
  • Keep thread colours few. Logos with a handful of solid colours stitch best. Gradients turn into solid bands of thread.
  • Share your brand colours. Pantone or hex codes help us pick the closest thread shade, which you will see on the mockup.
  • Mind the size. A left chest logo usually sits around 3 to 4 inches wide. Cap fronts are smaller and curved, so a very wide logo may need a stacked version.
  • Plan the contrast. Gold or white thread on navy, navy on white and black on grey all read clearly from across a lobby.

Fabric pairing for embroidery

Embroidery likes a stable fabric. Matty and polycot polos are favourites for hotel and service teams, because they hold their shape and support the stitches well. Cotton and polycot corporate shirts take a chest logo beautifully, and caps in cotton or polycot give a firm surface for a front logo. Sinker hoodies, varsity jackets and track jackets carry larger embroidery with ease.

Light, stretchy tees are trickier. A small chest logo works fine, but a large embroidered design on a thin tee can pull the fabric. If you want stitched logos on t-shirts, keep the logo small and choose a slightly heavier tee. Share your preferred GSM and we will confirm availability.

The sea air in Juhu and long shifts near the airport also matter. Polycot wrinkles less and dries faster than pure cotton, which staff appreciate on twelve hour days.

Durability and care for embroidered uniforms

Embroidery is among the most durable ways to brand a garment. There is no print layer to fade or peel, and good thread holds its colour through a great deal of washing. That is why so many hotels and offices prefer it for uniforms that are worn almost every day.

A few simple habits keep embroidered pieces looking fresh:

  • Wash inside out on a gentle cycle in cool or lukewarm water.
  • Avoid bleach, which can change thread colours.
  • Squeeze gently rather than wringing the embroidered area hard.
  • Iron on the reverse side, with a cloth over the logo.
  • Hand wash caps or spot clean them, so they keep their shape.
