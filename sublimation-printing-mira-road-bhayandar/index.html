How sublimation printing works, step by step

Sublimation uses heat to turn special ink into a gas that bonds with polyester fibres. It sounds like science class, and it is a little bit, but the result is simple: a design that becomes part of the fabric itself. Here is the process at our unit once you approve your mockup:

  1. Printing the design on paper. Your artwork is printed in full colour, as a mirror image, onto special sublimation paper using sublimation inks.
  2. Placing the paper on the fabric. The printed sheet is laid carefully on white or light polyester, lined up with the panel or garment.
  3. Pressing with heat. A heat press applies high temperature and pressure, which turns the ink into a gas.
  4. Bonding into the fibres. The gas enters the opened up polyester fibres and locks in as they cool, so the colour sits inside the fabric, not on it.
  5. Finishing. The paper is removed, the piece is checked, and it moves on to finishing and packing.

Because the ink becomes part of the fibre, you cannot feel a sublimated print with your fingers. Our main page on sublimation printing in Mumbai covers the method in more depth.

When to choose sublimation, and when another method fits better

Sublimation is brilliant within its limits, and those limits are very clear. Here is how to decide.

Sublimation is the right choice when:

  • The garment is polyester, with a white or light base.
  • You want all-over designs, patterns, gradients or photos that run across the whole piece.
  • The garment will be worn for sport and washed often.
  • You want a print you cannot feel, with no layer on top of the fabric.

Choose another method when:

  • You want cotton tees or cotton jerseys. Sublimation does not work on cotton at all, so DTF printing in Mira Road is the better route for full colour designs on cotton.
  • The garment itself is dark coloured, like a ready black polyester tee. Sublimation needs a white or light base fabric.
  • You want a raised, stitched crest on a track jacket or polo for a premium club look. Embroidery in Mira Road is made for that.

Here is a useful trick if your team wants a dark kit. Since the design can cover the whole garment, a navy or black jersey can be created by printing the dark colour onto white polyester. You get the dark look with all the benefits of sublimation.

Designing artwork for sublimation

Sublimation can print almost anything you can imagine, but a few rules help your kit look its best.

  • Go big and bold. Full colour, gradients, photos and patterns all work. This is the one method where an edge to edge design costs no more than a small one in the same position.
  • Remember there is no white ink. Any white in your design shows the fabric colour. On white polyester, that is perfect. On a light coloured base, white areas will show that colour instead.
  • Extend your background. For all-over designs, let the background run past the garment edges so there are no gaps near seams.
  • Make numbers readable. Use high contrast between numbers and the background, so they are easy to read from the boundary.
  • Send sponsor logos in high resolution. Vector files such as PDF or AI are best. Blurry logos look blurrier when printed large.
  • Prepare a clean player list. Send name, number and size for each player in one sheet, and check spellings before sending. We match it to the mockup before production starts.

Fabric pairing: why sublimation needs polyester

Sublimation works only on polyester, because the ink bonds with polyester fibres when heated. Cotton fibres do not take the dye in the same way, so the print would wash out. The base fabric also needs to be white or light, because the dye is transparent and cannot cover a dark colour.

The good news is that polyester is exactly what you want for sport in Mira Road's coastal climate. It dries quickly, stays light when you sweat, and does not cling the way a damp cotton tee does. A light base with a bright design also stays cooler under the sun on an open ground than a dark fabric would. If your team has a fabric weight in mind, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Because the design lives inside the fibres, a sublimated print does not crack, peel or fade the way surface prints can. It also keeps the fabric breathable, since there is no layer sitting on top. With basic care, your kit stays match ready season after season.

  • Rinse or wash soon after a sweaty game, rather than leaving the kit in a bag overnight.
  • Wash in cold water with a mild detergent, inside out if you like.
  • Avoid bleach and strong stain removers.
  • Dry in the shade on a hanger. Polyester dries fast, even in the monsoon.
  • Avoid ironing at high heat, which can soften the colours. A low setting from the reverse is enough if needed.

Treat it well, and the electric blue waves will still be turning heads at next year's tournament.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Mira Road?

Sublimation printing starts at ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. A sports jersey starts at ₹200, so a jersey printed front and back starts at ₹250. The final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp once we see your design.

Can sublimation be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation works only on polyester, because the ink bonds with polyester fibres under heat. For full colour designs on cotton, we use DTF printing instead.

Can I get a black or navy sublimated jersey in Bhayandar?

Yes, by printing the dark colour as part of the design. The base fabric must be white or light polyester, but the design can cover it fully in navy, black or any colour you like. A ready dark polyester garment cannot be sublimated.

What is the minimum order for sublimated jerseys in Mira Road?

The minimum order for sports jerseys, shorts and tees is 5 pieces. That makes sublimation easy even for a small five-a-side team.

Do sublimated jerseys crack or fade after washing?

No. The design is dyed into the polyester fibres, so there is no surface layer to crack or peel. Wash in cold water, dry in the shade and avoid high heat ironing to keep colours bright.

Can each jersey have a different name and number?

Yes. Names and numbers are part of the sublimated design, so every jersey can be different. Send a list with each player's name, number and size, and we check it against the mockup before production.

Is sublimation or DTF better for football jerseys?

For polyester football jerseys, sublimation is usually better, because the design becomes part of the fabric and stays light and breathable. DTF is the better choice for cotton jerseys or dark ready made fabric. Both can carry names and numbers.
