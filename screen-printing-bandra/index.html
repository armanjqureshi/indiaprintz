How screen printing works, in plain words

Screen printing is one of the oldest ways of putting ink on fabric, and the basic idea has stayed the same for good reason. It is simple, sturdy and very efficient once it is set up.

  1. Your design is separated into colours. A 2 colour logo becomes two separate layers, one for each ink.
  2. A screen is prepared for each colour. Each screen is a fine mesh stretched on a frame, coated so that ink can pass only through the shape of that colour.
  3. The garment is placed on a flat bed. It is lined up so every piece in the run gets the print in the same spot.
  4. Ink is pulled through the screen with a squeegee. One firm pass presses the ink through the open mesh and onto the fabric.
  5. The next colour follows, perfectly aligned. For multi-colour designs, each screen is registered so the layers sit exactly where they should.
  6. The print is heat cured. Curing sets the ink so it bonds with the fabric and survives washing.

The effort goes into preparing the screens. Once they are ready, printing each extra shirt is quick, which is why the cost per piece drops so nicely on larger runs. Our page on screen printing in Mumbai has more on ink types and print sizes across the city.

When to choose screen printing, and when to pick another method

Every method has a sweet spot. Screen printing has a very clear one, and stepping outside it usually means another method will serve you better.

Screen printing is the smart choice when:

  • You are ordering roughly 50 pieces or more of the same design.
  • Your artwork uses 1 to 3 flat colours, like most logos and event graphics.
  • You are printing on cotton, bio-wash, sinker or polycot.
  • You want a bold, solid print with a slightly raised, inky feel.
  • Every shirt in the order carries the same design.

Another method is better when:

  • Your design is a photograph, a gradient or has many colours. DTF reproduces these without extra setup, and our page on DTF printing in Bandra shows how it handles small and detailed jobs.
  • You need only 10 or 20 pieces. At that size, DTF usually costs less overall.
  • Each shirt needs a different name or number.
  • You want a full polyester sports kit with an all-over design. That is a job for sublimation printing in Bandra.
  • You want a stitched logo on a corporate polo, where embroidery gives a more premium finish.

A clever trick for large orders is to combine methods. A fest committee might screen print the main design on 300 tees and use DTF for the names on the backs of a small core team.

Artwork and file tips for clean screen prints

Screen printing loves clear, confident artwork. A little preparation on your side makes the screens sharper and the final print cleaner.

  • Send a vector file if you can. AI, PDF, EPS or SVG files let us separate colours precisely. A high resolution PNG also works, and we will flag anything that needs redrawing.
  • Count your colours. Each colour is a separate screen. A 2 colour design is simpler and cheaper to set up than a 4 colour one, so trimming a colour can be worth it.
  • Avoid gradients and soft shadows. Screen printing lays down solid ink, so smooth fades either need special halftone work or are better done with DTF.
  • Name your ink colours. If your brand has exact colours, share the Pantone references so we can match them as closely as possible.
  • Mind the garment colour. Light inks on dark fabric may need an underbase. Tell us the tee colour up front so the quote and mockup reflect it.
  • Keep fine lines reasonable. Very thin strokes and tiny text can fill in with ink. Slightly bolder lines print far more reliably.

When the mockup arrives, check the print size against the garment. A design that looks perfect on screen can feel too small on an XXL back, so ask us to scale it if needed.

Fabric pairing for screen printing

Screen printing ink grips natural fibres especially well, which is why cotton is its natural partner.

Cotton is the classic. The ink soaks into the fibres and cures into a durable, comfortable print. It is our usual suggestion for fair, fest and society tees, especially in humid Mumbai weather.

Bio-wash cotton has a smoother surface that gives crisp edges, making it a good pick for label drops where the shirt needs to feel premium.

Sinker has a slightly heavier hand and holds bold prints beautifully on everyday tees and hoodies.

Polycot blends cotton and polyester for shape and colour retention, a sensible choice for staff tees and NSS polos that get washed often.

Pure polyester is possible with the right inks, but for sports kits on polyester, sublimation usually gives a better result. If a particular weight matters to you, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for screen printed tees

A properly cured screen print is one of the toughest finishes in garment printing, because the ink is set into the fibres rather than resting on top. A few easy habits keep it looking bright.

  • Wash the garment inside out, with similar colours.
  • Use cold or lukewarm water and a mild detergent.
  • Keep bleach well away from printed areas.
  • Dry in the shade rather than harsh afternoon sun.
  • Iron on the reverse side, never directly on the ink.

For volunteer tees that might be worn every year at the same fair, these steps genuinely add seasons to the shirt.

Frequently asked questions

How much does screen printing cost in Bandra?

Screen printing starts from ₹15 per print position, added to the garment price. A round neck tee with one screen printed position starts at ₹135 per piece. The final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp once we see your design and quantity.

Is screen printing cheaper than DTF for 100 tees?

Usually, yes, if your design has 1 to 3 flat colours. At 100 pieces, the screen setup is spread across every shirt, and the per position price starts at ₹15 compared with ₹35 for DTF.

What is the minimum order for screen printing in Bandra?

Most products start from 5 pieces. Screen printing becomes most economical from about 50 pieces upward, so for smaller quantities we often suggest DTF.

How many colours can you screen print on a t-shirt?

Screen printing works best with 1 to 3 flat colours. Designs with photos, gradients or many colours are better suited to DTF printing.

Can you screen print tees for a college fest at St Andrew's or National College?

Yes. College fests are classic screen printing jobs, with large quantities and one bold design. Send the design and a size split on WhatsApp, and we will share a quote and dispatch date.

Does screen printing work on polyester sports t-shirts?

It can, but cotton and blends give the best result. For polyester sports kits, especially with all-over designs, sublimation is usually the better method.

How long does a screen print last on a t-shirt?

A well cured screen print is very durable and handles regular washing well. Wash inside out in cold or lukewarm water, avoid bleach and iron on the reverse to keep it bright.
