Cricket jersey printing for Shivaji Park clubs

There is something special about a club in proper whites or colours with a crest on the chest. Most clubs want a coloured jersey with the club logo, a sponsor on the front, and each player's name and number on the back. For that, sublimation is the method we recommend almost every time.

Why sublimation suits cricket jerseys

Sublimation dyes the design into the polyester fabric instead of laying it on top. That means the print does not crack, peel or fade in the wash, and the jersey stays light and breathable through a long day in the field. It also lets you go wild with stripes, panels, gradients and all-over patterns at a sensible cost.

The one thing to know is that sublimation only works on polyester with a white or light base. If your club prefers cotton, we switch to DTF or screen printing and talk you through the difference. Our page on custom cricket and sports jersey printing has more ideas for kit design.

Getting names and numbers right

Every jersey in a kit is different, so we work from a simple list: name, number and size for each player. Send it as a typed message or a spreadsheet rather than a photo of a handwritten sheet, because that is where spelling mistakes creep in. We then show you the names on the mockup so you can check every one.

Collect the size split from your volunteer list before you order. Mandals that guess sizes often run short in L and XL, and finding extra pieces in festival week is stressful for everyone. Choosing a print method for your Dadar order

The print method is added per print position, per piece. A print position is one print area, such as the front chest, the full back or a sleeve, so front plus back counts as 2 positions.

  • Screen printing, from ₹15: the cheapest per piece on runs of about 50 and above, for 1 to 3 flat colours. Best on cotton and blends, and a favourite for mandal and fest tees.
  • DTF printing, from ₹35: full colour, photos and gradients with no setup. Lovely for small quantities and individual names, and it works on most fabrics.
  • Embroidery, from ₹45: stitched logos for polos, shirts, caps and jackets. Very fine text and photographs cannot be embroidered.
  • Sublimation, from ₹25: dyes the design into polyester only, on white or light fabric. Ideal for all-over designs, jerseys and kits, and it does not work on cotton.

Torn between the two most popular options? Our guide to DTF vs screen printing compares them on cost and finish.

Fabrics and sizing made simple

Cotton is soft and comfortable for daily wear and takes screen printing well. Polyester is light, dries quickly and is the only fabric for sublimation. Polycot is a cotton and polyester blend that holds its shape, sinker and bio-wash are softer cotton finishes, matty has a textured weave often used for polos, and dry-fit is the sports fabric in our NSS polos.

If you have a particular fabric weight in mind, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability with the quote.

Our size chart uses garment measurements in inches. Chest for S, M, L, XL and XXL is 36, 40, 44, 48 and 52, length is 26, 27, 28, 29 and 30, and sleeve is 8, 8.5, 9, 9.5 and 10. Lay a t-shirt that already fits you well on a table, measure it, and compare before you send your size split.

How much does t-shirt printing cost in Dadar?

Round neck t-shirts start at ₹120 per piece before printing, and screen printing starts at ₹15 per position. So 50 tees printed front and back start at (120 + 15 x 2) x 50 = ₹7,500. The final price depends on fabric, method and quantity, and we confirm it on WhatsApp.

What is the best print for cricket jerseys at Shivaji Park?

Sublimation on polyester is the best choice for cricket jerseys. It dyes the design into the fabric, so names, numbers and sponsor logos do not crack or peel. It does not work on cotton, so a cotton kit would use DTF or screen printing instead.
