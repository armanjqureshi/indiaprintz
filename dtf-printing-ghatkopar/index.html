What DTF printing is and how it works

DTF stands for direct to film. Instead of printing straight onto the fabric, your design is first printed onto a thin film and then pressed onto the garment with heat. It sounds technical, but the process is easy to picture.

  1. The artwork is prepared. We size your design for each print position and check the colours against the mockup you approved.
  2. The design is printed on film. A special printer lays down the colour inks on clear film and then adds a layer of white ink behind them. That white base is what keeps colours bright on a black or navy tee.
  3. Adhesive powder goes on. A fine powder is spread over the wet ink and melted, so the print can bond to fabric.
  4. The film is pressed onto the garment. A heat press fuses the print to the tee, hoodie or bag, and the film is peeled away.
  5. A final press sets it. This last press softens the surface and locks the print into the weave.

Because each print comes from a film rather than a screen, there is no setup cost per design. That is why ten tees with ten different names cost the same as ten identical tees, which is lovely news for anyone planning a group order. When DTF is the right pick, and when another method wins

Every method has its sweet spot, and choosing well saves money and gives a better finish. DTF is your best friend when:

  • Your design has many colours, a photo, a gradient or soft shading.
  • You need a different name or number on each piece.
  • Your quantity is small, from a handful of pieces up to a few dozen.
  • The garment is dark and the colours still need to pop.
  • You want to reorder small top-ups later without a new setup.

There are times we will happily point you elsewhere. If your design is one or two flat colours and you need 100 or more tees, screen printing in Ghatkopar usually works out cheaper per piece and feels softer on large areas. For football kits and all-over garba prints on white polyester, sublimation printing in Ghatkopar dyes colour into the fabric so nothing sits on top. Artwork and file tips for sharp DTF prints

DTF reproduces almost anything, which means it also reproduces mistakes faithfully. A few minutes spent on your file makes a real difference.

  • Send the highest quality file you have. A PNG with a transparent background at 300 dpi at print size is ideal. Logos are best as vector files such as AI, PDF or SVG.
  • Avoid fading edges into nothing. Soft glows and shadows that fade to transparent can print with a hard edge. Ask us and we will suggest a cleaner version.
  • Keep fine lines thick enough to see. Hairline strokes and tiny text can break up. If you need a magnifying glass to read it on screen, it is too small for a tee.
  • Break up very large solid blocks. A huge solid rectangle sits on the fabric like a patch. Artwork with some breathing gaps feels softer on warm Mumbai days.
  • Send names as one clean list. Spell every name exactly as it should print, with the size next to it.
  • Check colours on the garment colour. Bright yellow on white and bright yellow on black look different, and the mockup shows you the real result.

Fabrics that pair well with DTF

One of the best things about DTF is how forgiving it is with fabric. It bonds well with cotton, polyester, sinker, polycot and bio-wash, and it sits nicely on the canvas of a tote bag too.

Cotton round necks and hoodies are the classic pairing for fests and family events, soft against the skin with prints that stay vivid. Polycot is a smart pick for office tees because it holds its shape through regular washing. Polyester works for sporty tees and hi-vis jackets, where a logo needs to stay readable on a bright base.

The one place we gently steer people away from DTF is a fully printed sports kit. On stretchy polyester jerseys meant for hours of running, sublimation breathes better because it adds no layer at all. For everything else, DTF is a safe and colourful choice. If you have a fabric weight in mind, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

How long DTF prints last, and how to wash them

A good DTF print is flexible, so it moves with the fabric and does not crack when you stretch the tee over your head. With a little care it stays bright through many washes, often outlasting the garment's own colour.

  • Turn the garment inside out before washing.
  • Use cold or lukewarm water with a mild detergent, and skip bleach.
  • Avoid wringing hard around the print.
  • Dry in the shade rather than strong sun, and skip high heat in a dryer.
  • Never iron directly on the print. Iron inside out, or place a cloth over it.
  • Wait a day after delivery before the first wash so the print settles fully.

Garba tees in particular face sweat, dust and late nights, so a gentle wash the morning after keeps them ready for the next round.

Can every DTF tee have a different name?

Yes, and it costs the same as printing identical tees. Since DTF needs no setup per design, each name is simply printed on its own film. Send the names in one list with sizes next to them.

Does DTF work on black and dark t-shirts?

It does. A white ink layer goes behind your colours, so they stay bright on black, navy and maroon. The mockup will show you how your colours look on the garment colour you chose.
