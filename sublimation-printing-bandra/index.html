How sublimation printing works

Sublimation sounds scientific, and in a way it is. The idea is simple once you see the steps.

  1. The design is printed onto transfer paper. Special sublimation dyes are printed in full colour, as a mirror image, onto paper.
  2. The paper is placed on the polyester fabric or garment panel. For all-over designs, the panels are often printed before the jersey is stitched together.
  3. Heat and pressure are applied. In a heat press, the dye turns into a gas without becoming liquid first.
  4. The gas bonds with the polyester fibres. The fibres open slightly under heat, take in the dye, and then close around it as they cool.
  5. The paper is removed. The design is now inside the fabric, with no layer on top that could crack or peel.

Because the dye becomes part of the fibre, you cannot feel the print at all. Run your hand across a sublimated jersey and it feels like plain fabric. For more on what this method can do across the city, see our page on sublimation printing in Mumbai.

When to choose sublimation, and when another method is better

Sublimation is a specialist. Inside its lane it is unbeatable. Outside it, it simply does not work, so it helps to know the boundaries.

Sublimation is perfect when:

  • The garment is polyester, white or light coloured.
  • You want an all-over design, edge to edge, including patterns, gradients and photos.
  • The kit is for sport or fitness, where lightness and breathability matter.
  • You want names and numbers that will not crack or peel.

Pick another method when:

  • The garment is cotton. Sublimation does not work on cotton, because the dye has nothing to bond with.
  • The fabric is black, navy or another dark colour. The dye is transparent and will not show on dark fabric.
  • You want 100 or more simple cotton tees for a fest or event. That is a job for screen printing in Bandra.
  • You want a stitched crest on a coach's polo or a team cap. Embroidery in Bandra gives that premium touch.

Some teams combine methods. A cricket club might sublimate its playing kits and order embroidered polos for coaches and managers, keeping the whole squad in matching colours.

Designing artwork for sublimation

Sublimation can print almost anything, including photos, gradients, patterns and fine detail. A few design habits help kits look their best.

  • Design in full colour, at high resolution. Use 300 dpi at actual print size, or vector artwork for logos and numbers.
  • Plan for the seams. On all-over designs, important elements like logos and numbers should sit away from side seams, armholes and collars.
  • Keep the base light. Colours print best on a white base. Very dark design areas work, but they are created by dye, so plan your darkest colours carefully.
  • Avoid pure white details on coloured fabric. Sublimation cannot print white. White areas in your design will show the fabric colour underneath.
  • Send names and numbers as a list. A simple sheet with each player's name, number and size avoids errors.
  • Check contrast on numbers. Back numbers should be readable from the stands, so pick colours with strong contrast.

If you are not sure about your design, send your idea on WhatsApp. We will help you translate it into a mockup on the actual jersey shape.

Fabric pairing for sublimation

Sublimation has one non-negotiable rule: it needs polyester. The dye bonds with polyester fibres, so the higher the polyester content, the brighter and sharper the result.

100 percent polyester gives the most vivid colours and is the standard for jerseys, shorts and running tees. It dries quickly and stays light during long matches.

White and pastel polyester are the best bases. Light grey, sky blue or cream work too, but the base colour mixes with the dye, so the mockup will show you the effect.

Cotton, sinker and bio-wash are not suitable for sublimation. If your team prefers cotton tees for off-field wear, we can print those with DTF or screen printing instead.

Polycot blends have only partial polyester content, which can give a faded, vintage look. For team kits, we usually recommend pure polyester. If you need a specific fabric weight, tell us the GSM you want and we confirm availability.

Durability and wash care for sublimated kits

Sublimated designs are some of the longest lasting decoration available. Because the dye is inside the fibre, there is nothing to crack, peel or flake. With simple care, kits stay bright through many seasons.

  • Wash in cold or lukewarm water after every match or session.
  • Turn the kit inside out to reduce rubbing.
  • Skip fabric softener, which can affect the breathability of polyester.
  • Dry in the shade and avoid the dryer on high heat.
  • Do not iron directly on printed areas, since high heat can affect polyester.

A good habit for teams is to air out sweaty kits straight after the match, even before washing. It keeps them fresh and protects the fabric.

Frequently asked questions

How much does sublimation printing cost in Bandra?

Sublimation printing starts from ₹25 per print position, added to the garment price. A sports jersey with one sublimated position starts at ₹225 per piece. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp after we see your design.

Can sublimation be done on cotton t-shirts?

No. Sublimation only works on polyester, because the dye bonds with polyester fibres. For cotton tees, DTF or screen printing is the right choice.

Can you sublimate a design on a black jersey?

Sublimation does not work on dark fabric, because the dye is transparent. It works best on white or light coloured polyester, where colours stay bright.

What is the minimum order for sublimated football jerseys in Bandra?

Sports jerseys start from 5 pieces. You can add names and numbers to each jersey at no extra setup.

Will sublimated names and numbers peel off?

No. The dye becomes part of the polyester fibre, so there is nothing on the surface to peel or crack. Wash in cold water and avoid high heat to keep the colours bright.

Can you make matching kits for a BKC office cricket league?

Is sublimation good for gym and running t-shirts?
