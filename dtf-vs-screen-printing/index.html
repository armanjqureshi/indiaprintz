DTF vs Screen Printing: Which Is Better for Your T-Shirt Order

DTF vs screen printing for custom t-shirts in Mumbai. Compare cost per piece, quantity, colours, feel and wash care, with worked prices for 10 to 300 tees.

By indiaprints and stitches · Updated 20 October 2026 · 12 min read

DTF vs screen printing is the first question to settle on almost every custom t-shirt order we quote for Mumbai, Thane and Navi Mumbai. Both methods give a sharp, lasting print, but they suit very different jobs. One is built for full colour artwork and small quantities, the other for simple designs on a big run.

We run our own cutting, stitching and printing unit in Dahanu, on the Western line north of Mumbai, and we have used both methods on corporate and personal orders for more than 8 years. This guide explains how each one works, what it costs per t-shirt using our starting prices, and how to pick the right one for your design and quantity.

If you already know your design and quantity, you can skip to the checklist near the end. Otherwise, start with how the two methods actually put ink on fabric, because that explains almost every difference in price and finish.

How DTF and screen printing work

What is DTF printing

DTF stands for direct to film. The design is printed in full colour onto a special transfer film, then heat pressed onto the garment. The steps look like this:

  1. Your artwork is printed onto PET film with colour inks plus a white base layer, so colours stay bright even on black or navy fabric.
  2. A fine adhesive powder is applied to the wet print and cured with heat.
  3. The film is placed on the t-shirt and pressed under heat and pressure.
  4. The film is peeled away, leaving the design bonded to the fabric surface.

Because nothing has to be set up per colour, DTF costs the same to start whether your design has two colours or two hundred. That is why it handles photos, gradients and individual names easily. You can see more on our DTF printing service page.

How screen printing works

Screen printing pushes ink through a fine mesh screen directly onto the fabric. Each colour in the design needs its own screen, so the process is:

  1. The design is separated into its flat colours, usually 1 to 3 for t-shirt work.
  2. A screen is prepared for each colour, with the design area left open in the mesh.
  3. The t-shirt is loaded on the press and ink is pulled across each screen with a squeegee, one colour at a time.
  4. The printed garment is heat cured so the ink sets into the fabric.

The screens take work to prepare, and that setup is spread across every piece in the run. On 300 tees it adds very little per piece. On 10 tees it adds a lot. Our screen printing service page has more detail on colours and print sizes.

DTF printing cost vs screen printing cost per t-shirt

All the examples below use round neck t-shirts from ₹120 and this formula: (garment price + method price x positions) x quantity. These are starting prices only. Your final quote is confirmed on WhatsApp once we see the design, fabric and sizes.

One important point. Screen printing setup is spread across the run, and more colours mean more screens, so a 3 colour design costs more than a 1 colour design. The screen printing figures below are the starting point for a simple design. On very small runs, the real screen price per piece will come out higher once the setup is spread over so few pieces.

10 t-shirts, front print only

  • DTF: (120 + 35 x 1) x 10 = ₹1,550
  • Screen: (120 + 15 x 1) x 10 = ₹1,350 as a starting figure

On paper screen looks cheaper, but with only 10 pieces the screen setup has almost nothing to spread over. For 10 tees, DTF is nearly always the sensible choice, and it lets you print photos or full colour logos with no extra effort.

50 t-shirts, front and back

  • DTF: (120 + 35 x 2) x 50 = ₹9,500
  • Screen: (120 + 15 x 2) x 50 = ₹7,500

Around 50 pieces is where screen printing starts to pay off for a 1 to 3 colour design. If your design has a photo or a gradient, DTF is still the answer even at this quantity.

100 t-shirts, front print only

  • DTF: (120 + 35 x 1) x 100 = ₹15,500
  • Screen: (120 + 15 x 1) x 100 = ₹13,500

At 100 pieces the gap is clear for simple logos and slogans. This is a typical size for company events, college fests and society functions, and it is where many of our bulk orders sit.

300 t-shirts, front and back

  • DTF: (120 + 35 x 2) x 300 = ₹57,000
  • Screen: (120 + 15 x 2) x 300 = ₹45,000

On a run of 300, the screen setup is a tiny share of each piece and the difference adds up to ₹12,000 on starting prices. Large staff drives, marathons and induction kits are usually screen printed for exactly this reason.

Which is better for t-shirts: DTF or screen

Neither method is better in every case. The right one depends on four things: quantity, number of colours, whether every piece is the same, and the fabric.

When to choose DTF

  • You need fewer than about 50 pieces.
  • The design has a photo, a gradient, fine detail or many colours.
  • Every t-shirt needs a different name, number or role.
  • The fabric is polyester, sinker or a blend where screen ink may not sit as well.
  • You want a sample or a small first batch before a bigger reorder.

For one-off gifts and very small orders, our custom printed t-shirt in full colour starts at ₹200 with a minimum of just 2 pieces.

When to choose screen printing

  • You need about 50 pieces or more of the same design.
  • The design is 1 to 3 flat colours, such as a logo, a slogan or a simple illustration.
  • The t-shirts are cotton or a cotton blend.
  • You want the lowest cost per piece on a large run.

Most corporate t-shirts for company events fall into this group, since a single colour logo on the chest and a slogan on the back is a very common brief.

Using both methods on one order

You do not have to pick one method for the whole garment. A popular setup for teams, college committees and office sports days is to screen print the common design on the front of every t-shirt and add each person's name on the back with DTF.

Here is how that works on 50 round neck tees with a screen printed front and a DTF name on the back: (120 + 15 + 35) x 50 = ₹8,500 at starting prices. You get the low screen price on the part that repeats and the flexibility of DTF on the part that changes. Send us the name list in a spreadsheet or a simple WhatsApp message with sizes, and we print and pack each piece labelled by size.

Where sublimation and embroidery fit

DTF and screen printing cover most t-shirt work, but two other methods come up often.

Sublimation, from ₹25 per position, dyes the design into polyester fabric. It only works on white or light polyester and does not work on cotton. It is the right choice for all-over designs, sports jerseys and team kits, where the print becomes part of the fabric. See our sublimation printing page for fabric and colour details.

Embroidery, from ₹45 per position, stitches the logo into the garment. It looks premium on polos, shirts, caps and jackets, but very fine text and photos cannot be embroidered. Many companies pair embroidered polos for staff with screen printed t-shirts for events. Our embroidery service page covers thread colours and logo sizes.

Artwork file tips for each method

The right file saves a round of corrections and keeps your mockup accurate.

Files for screen printing

Send a vector file where possible: AI, EPS, SVG or a PDF exported from a design program. Vector artwork scales cleanly and can be separated into flat colours for the screens. Tell us the exact colours you want, and if you have brand colour codes, share them.

If you only have a JPG of your logo, send it anyway. We will tell you whether it can be cleaned up for screen printing or whether DTF is the better route.

Files for DTF printing

Send a high resolution PNG with a transparent background, sized close to the print size you want. A transparent background matters because anything left in the file, including a white box around the logo, will be printed. Photos should be sharp at full size, not screenshots or images taken from social media.

DTF print durability and wash care

Both methods last well when the t-shirt is looked after. The care steps are slightly different because DTF sits on the surface while screen ink sets into the fabric.

Caring for DTF printed t-shirts

  • Wait a day or two before the first wash.
  • Wash inside out in cold or normal water on a gentle cycle.
  • Do not iron directly on the print. Iron inside out or place a cloth over the design.
  • Avoid high heat in a tumble dryer. Line drying in shade is best.
  • Do not use bleach or strong stain removers on the printed area.

Treated this way, DTF prints stay bright and flexible. Most cracking or peeling we see on DTF comes from direct ironing or very hot drying.

Caring for screen printed t-shirts

  • Wash inside out to protect the ink surface.
  • Normal machine or hand wash is fine.
  • Avoid bleach.
  • Iron inside out, not directly on the print.

Screen prints on cotton are very hard wearing and usually last as long as the t-shirt itself. Fabric choice matters too, and our custom t-shirt printing page lists the fabrics we work with for each style.

A quick DTF vs screen printing checklist

Run through these questions before you send your design. If most of your answers point one way, that is your method.

  1. Quantity: under about 50 pieces points to DTF, 50 and above points to screen.
  2. Colours: photos, gradients or more than 3 colours point to DTF. 1 to 3 flat colours point to screen.
  3. Personalisation: different names or numbers on each piece point to DTF, or to a mix of screen and DTF.
  4. Fabric: cotton and cotton blends suit screen. Polyester and mixed fabrics usually suit DTF, or sublimation for all-over jersey prints.
  5. Budget per piece: on large simple runs, screen gives the lowest price.
  6. Mixed needs: if the design repeats but names change, use screen for the common print and DTF for the names.

If you are unsure, send the design anyway. We will suggest the method that gives the best result for your budget, and explain why.

Frequently asked questions

Is DTF or screen printing better for t-shirts?

It depends on quantity and design. DTF is better for small orders, full colour artwork, photos and individual names. Screen printing is better and cheaper for about 50 pieces or more of a simple 1 to 3 colour design on cotton.

What is the DTF printing cost per t-shirt in Mumbai?

DTF starts at ₹35 per print position, added to the garment price. A round neck t-shirt from ₹120 with a front DTF print starts at ₹155 per piece. The final price is confirmed on WhatsApp once we see your design and quantity.

What is the screen printing cost per t-shirt?

Screen printing starts at ₹15 per print position. On 100 round neck tees with a front print, the starting price is (120 + 15) x 100 = ₹13,500. Designs with more colours cost more, because each colour needs its own screen.

What is the screen printing minimum order?

The minimum order is set by the garment, which is 5 pieces for round neck t-shirts. Screen printing becomes good value from about 50 pieces, because the setup is spread across the run. For smaller quantities we usually recommend DTF.

How long does a DTF print last?

A DTF print lasts well when the t-shirt is washed inside out, not ironed directly on the print and not dried on high heat. Most damage comes from ironing over the design. Screen prints on cotton are generally the most hard wearing of the two.

Can I print different names on each t-shirt?

Yes, with DTF. Each piece can carry its own name, number or role at the same starting price per position. Many teams screen print the common front design and add names on the back with DTF.

Does DTF work on polyester t-shirts?

Yes, DTF works on most fabrics including polyester, sinker, polycot and bio-wash. Screen printing works best on cotton and blends. For all-over prints on white or light polyester jerseys, sublimation is often the better choice.

What file should I send for t-shirt printing?

For screen printing, send a vector file such as AI, EPS, SVG or PDF. For DTF, send a high resolution PNG with a transparent background. If you only have a JPG or a photo, send it on WhatsApp and we will tell you what can be done with it.
